Workspace Clarity Plan

What to change so the workspace page can be read at a glance and the builder rail can be scanned. Every recommendation is tied to the component and file it lands in, and all of it stays within plain HTML, CSS and JS. The screenshots and line references are from the mockup as it is today.

Status · built 2 November 2026

Where the build differs from this page: a product’s share of its category shows as a bare figure (“44%”), with “44.4% of Public Equity” in its tooltip, because “of category” cost the name too much width; the rail marks the selected row with its wash only, since a side bar would collide with the category colour; sleeve names in tags are no longer uppercase; the Drivers & differences table moves a product’s type and identifier into a tooltip on its name; and because Headline uses a factor model, the Factor model control shows whenever Headline is in the workspace.

Who reads this page

The spec (section 04) names three roles and one principle:

Portfolio managers primarily need rapid construction, understandable trade-offs, and stable comparisons. Analysts and researchers need precise assumptions, look-through detail, multi-factor inspection, and inspectable methodology. They use the same interface; the distinction is how far they drill.
PMPortfolio managerBuilds and edits. Needs the weight, the total, what changed, and the difference against the reference.
AnalystInvestment analystLook-through, coverage, methodology, factor detail, the data table behind a chart.
ResearcherResearcherScenarios, assumptions, projections, horizon and band.
Reader · assumedReaderOpens a colleague’s workspace to read it, not change it. Needs the headline and the comparison. Not in the spec; see decision 1.
The rule the calls follow

The default view is the PM’s and the reader’s view. Analyst and researcher detail is one interaction away, and where someone uses it all day, it can be switched on per workspace and stays on.

Why it feels busy

None of this is a single mistake. Each piece was added for a reason, and together they compete. The specifics, with where they come from:

  • Context is said many times. The basis appears in the context bar (context.js render), again in the context line (renderLine), again in the Drivers & differences subtitle (“Forward-looking · Platform CMA v2026.Q4”), and once more under each of the four column heads as “Platform model” (ddcard.js tableHtml, the gmeta span). Seven statements of one setting.
  • Default states are tagged. UI.kindTag (common.js:145) returns a “Live” tag for the default kind, so it appears on every live allocation in the strip, the rail head and the table heads. “Editing” is shown in the rail eyebrow, as a tag on the strip pill (which already has an accent ring) and as a tag on the table column (which already has an accent stripe). The header’s scenario button reads “Base case” when there is no scenario.
  • One style does a dozen jobs. .btn, .label, .eyebrow, .tag, .status, .tbl thead th, .rail-tab, .rail-eyebrow, .crumbs a, .act-tabs button, .menu-head and .sleeve-tag all use uppercase letter-spaced condensed type. Every button on the page shouts (ADD ALLOCATION, SNAPSHOT, EXPAND ALL, FIND PRODUCTS), so the style no longer signals anything.
  • Controls for things that are not on the page. Horizon and band are read only by the Projections card; the sample workspace has none, so two of the ten context-bar controls change nothing. Period only matters to historical cards. The bar shows everything regardless.
  • The same allocations are keyed three times above the fold. The comparison strip, the table column heads and the Cumulative return legend each list the four allocations with their colours, and the Allocations card adds a seven-category legend. Lower down, Active risk adds a fourth set of chips.
  • Explanations are permanent. Method notes sit under Drivers & differences (ddcard.js notes, two to four sentences), under most cards (cardtypes.js foot, activecard.js), and under the rail tree (“Weights are % of the total portfolio: type a value and press Enter…”).
  • Three levels of box, all bordered. The context bar has a border and shadow; each card has a border and shadow; each control inside them has a border. In the rail, 21 weight inputs are bordered boxes stacked in one column.
  • Chrome on every card at rest. Grip, model chip, settings icon and menu icon are always visible (cards.js cardHtml), and card subtitles pack four or five facts into one truncated line.
  • In the rail, categories and products share one row template. builder.js renderTree uses one row() for both levels: same 36px height, same subtitle style, same input, same menu. The differences are a 14px indent, an 8px colour square and a chevron, so category boundaries are hard to find.

Five rules that generate every call below

  1. Say it once. A setting is stated where it is set. A card repeats it only when the card departs from it.
  2. Tag the exception, not the default. Snapshot, Sleeve, Edited, Proxy, Coverage, No benchmark get a tag. Live, Platform model, Base case, Fully allocated do not.
  3. One style per job. Uppercase tracked type means a column head or a section eyebrow and nothing else. Bold means the figure that answers the question, once per level.
  4. Quiet until needed. Controls that act on a thing appear when the pointer or focus is on that thing. On touch screens they stay visible.
  5. Detail scales with space. A wider rail shows identifiers; a taller card shows its notes. Nothing is lost, it is reached.

Where roles disagree, and the resolution

1 · Method notes under cards
Analysts read them; PMs and readers scroll past them. Resolution: notes sit behind an “About these figures” <details> at the card foot, closed by default. A per-workspace preference, prefs.showNotes, opens every card’s notes and stays on; it lives in the header ⋮ menu as “Show method notes” and goes through S.setPref like Basis, saved and not undoable.
2 · Identifiers in the rail
Analysts scan tickers; PMs scan names and weights, and at 360px the ticker line already truncates the fact that matters. Resolution: the share of category is always shown; type and identifier appear when the rail is dragged to 420px or wider, in the row’s Inspect dialog, and in the Add tab’s search results. Nobody loses the information; the default width spends its pixels on names.
3 · The context in words
The spec asks for a context line that states the comparison in words, which suits a reader. Everyone else reads the bar. Resolution: remove the line. A quiet one-row bar with sentence-case labels reads as the sentence (“Forward-looking · ISG · Common history · vs Global 60/40”). If the reader role is confirmed, the export dialog gets the sentence as the cover line instead, where a reader actually needs it. The spec’s card-area requirement is updated to match.
4 · Per-column basis labels in the table
Analysts need to know a column is a backtest or has been edited; PMs want clean heads. Resolution: exception-only. The label is drawn when the columns differ from each other or a column is Edited or has a coverage caveat. On the forward basis with four platform-model columns, nothing is drawn; on the historical basis, “Actual history” against “Backtest” is drawn because it differs.
5 · Horizon and band
Researchers want them to hand; nobody else touches them. Resolution: they appear in the bar when a Projections card is in the workspace, and disappear when it is removed. The Projections card’s own subtitle keeps stating them.
6 · Snapshot in the header
PMs take snapshots at review points. Resolution: it moves into the ⋮ menu, the strip member menu and the rail menu, all of which already have it. If this proves too hidden, the fallback is a keyboard shortcut listed in the shortcuts dialog.

The one addition: a Headline card

Removing noise helps the eye find things; it does not put the answer first. Today the first card is the Drivers & differences table, nine rows tall, which is an analyst’s instrument. A PM or reader glancing at the page wants one line per allocation.

Headline is a card type in the catalogue (cardtypes.js), 12 columns by 4 rows, one row per compared allocation: expected return, risk, active return and tracking error against the reference, and the allocated total. It reuses the activeAnalysis request that the Active risk & return card already makes, so no new API method is needed; on the historical basis the same request returns realised figures. The sample workspace places it first, with the table second. It follows the context bar and has no settings of its own.

This is optional and is decision 6. Without it, the plan still removes the noise; with it, the page answers the question before it shows the working.

Decisions for you

1 · The reader role
I have assumed a fourth role who opens a workspace to read it. It only changes two calls: the export cover line (conflict 3) and the Headline card. Confirm or strike.
2 · Buttons in sentence case
This departs from the Proposal Tool’s uppercase buttons, which the tokens were copied from. The two products would still share colour, type and components, but not the button case. Default: sentence case here; the Proposal Tool is untouched.
3 · Remove the context line
The spec names it. Removing it means a spec change in the card-area requirement. Default: remove it.
4 · Method notes closed by default
Analysts opt in per workspace with “Show method notes”. The alternative is open by default with a “Hide notes” switch, which keeps today’s page. Default: closed.
5 · No Live tag, Editing shown once
Live allocations are unmarked; Snapshot and Sleeve keep their tags. “Editing” is the rail’s eyebrow and the accent ring on the strip pill, nowhere else. Default: yes.
6 · Add the Headline card and place it first
One new card type; the sample workspace reorders so it sits above the table. Default: add it.
7 · Identifiers only on a wide rail
Below 420px a product row shows name, share of category and weight. Type and identifier need the rail widened, or Inspect. Default: yes. The alternative is a rail setting “Show identifiers”, which adds a control to remove a line.
8 · Horizon and band only with a Projections card
They disappear from the bar when no card reads them. Default: yes.
