01 / Product intent

Make portfolio exploration immediate, legible, and comparable.

This platform helps portfolio managers, investment analysts, and researchers build and test new and existing multi-asset allocations. Its job is to expose historical and forward-looking return, risk, contributions, and exposures so that users can understand what drives an allocation and how it differs from its alternatives and benchmarks. Decisions remain with the user: the interface surfaces analytics, not investment recommendations or proposed trades.

Building from scratch, modifying an existing allocation, and comparing alternatives have equal product priority. They therefore share one persistent workspace rather than separate sequential workflows. The critical design test is whether a feature shortens the path from a question to a defensible comparison. Analytical depth should be reached through explicit controls, expansion, and selection—not by filling the initial view with every available dimension.

This document is a front-end specification. It defines visible behavior, interaction, state, and acceptance criteria, and it is accompanied by a working mockup (workspace/index.html) that implements every primary flow on fictional data. Numerical examples do not define production financial models or promise the accuracy of forecasts. Back-end model construction is outside scope, but model-provided units, status, provenance, and coverage must be represented faithfully.

One place to iterate

Edit the allocation tree on the left: portfolio → category → product. Inspect the analysis on the right as a collection of cards, with the Drivers & Differences table as the anchor card. The scenario drawer allows assumption editing alongside results.

Success: a user can edit a weight, inspect the changed contribution, and compare it with a benchmark without leaving the workspace.

Consistency before density

One column per allocation supports comparison across four to six alternatives. A shared context bar fixes what every card has in common—basis, factor model, period, reference, scenario, horizon—so that two cards never silently answer different questions. Factor exposure is an intentional exception: shared selected factors create grouped columns within each allocation.

Success: a user can explain what a displayed number means, its unit, period, reference, and scenario without opening another screen.

Depth through deliberate action

Expandable categories, index-level remainders, a card catalogue, per-card settings and a common factor picker expose detail on request. The large-desktop target permits information density, but does not remove the need for clear hierarchy.

Success: expanding a category, adding a card or changing a card’s metric does not reset comparison membership or force the user to rebuild their context.

Evidence, not advice

Differences, concentrations, contribution totals, and uncertainty are analytical outputs. The interface may describe them factually; it must not rank allocations as “best,” suggest a trade, or offer a recommended rebalance.

Success: every insight links to an inspectable value or view; no “Apply recommendation” action is present.

Read the workspace mockup as the primary product surface. The smaller examples below isolate interactions; they are not additional mandatory screens.

02 / Decision record

Confirmed direction and the status of this specification

Confirmed means the user explicitly selected that behavior, in the interview of 1 November 2026 or in the review of the second mockup iteration on 2 November 2026. Proposed means a predicted preference or a necessary implementation detail that has not been independently confirmed. The instruction to develop this document authorizes a complete design proposal; it does not silently convert every prediction into a confirmed requirement.

Confirmed foundation

  • Single workspace; adjustable builder/analytics split; very large desktop monitor as primary target.
  • Direct working drafts with continuous autosave and live recalculation.
  • The builder is a tree: portfolio → category → product. Categories are the platform’s fixed list; inline numeric editing; incomplete allocations allowed.
  • Shorts and leverage are opt-in per portfolio. Once enabled, there are no leverage risk limits; exposure and borrowing are informational.
  • Blank, template, and clone/import creation choices; universal investment search.
  • A coherent multi-asset framework, with historical and forward-looking analytics given equal access.

Confirmed in the second iteration (2 November 2026)

  • Index-level modelling. A category carries its own target weight; products implement it; the unimplemented remainder is modelled by the category’s index basket and shown as an index row.
  • Sleeves are saved subsets. A sleeve is a named, versioned set of products with weights summing to 100, created from a category or from a product selection, filed under a category in one shared repository, and applied to a category as a copy.
  • The analysis is a collection of cards from a card repository, on a free-form grid the user drags and resizes. The integrated table is one card. A context bar holds what every card shares.
  • Cards keep their own settings and can follow the selected table row. Saved cards store type, settings and name, shared across workspaces.

Confirmed analytical core

  • Drivers and Differences together in an integrated table.
  • Four to six allocations; live portfolios and fixed snapshots; shared and portfolio-specific benchmarks.
  • Contributions/Differences and Historical/Forward-looking switches.
  • Expandable category rows; weight, return contribution, risk contribution, and factor exposure available.
  • Several factors together, chosen once and shared across all allocations.
  • Switchable reference mode; risk displayed as share of total risk or contribution to volatility.
  • Scenario columns show Scenario and Δ vs Base; the editable weight is the total-portfolio weight; an edited allocation with actual history becomes a current-weight backtest, labelled.

Confirmed scenario and chart behavior

  • Editable capital-market assumptions at asset-class, factor, and holding levels in a scenario drawer.
  • Spreadsheet-style assumptions matrix and hybrid correlation editing.
  • Base and scenario results side by side, with selectable scope defaulting to all compared allocations.
  • Chart cards keep every compared allocation visible, with a selected set emphasized through the legend.
  • Projection cards offer Standalone growth and Portfolio contribution; central projection and bands in separate small charts for emphasized allocations.
  • Scenarios autosave within the workspace; no cross-workspace scenario library.

Superseded or interpreted choices

The first iteration’s builder used user-defined sleeves (Growth, Defensive, Diversifiers) as the branches of the tree and a single chart below a single table. Both are superseded: branches are now the fixed categories, “sleeve” means a saved, reusable subset of products, and the chart is one of several cards. The first iteration’s requirements that survive are restated below in the new vocabulary.

The earlier four-zone overview—Attractiveness, Drivers, Differences, and Actions—is superseded by the Drivers & Differences table. Recommendations and specific change proposals are explicitly excluded.

The earlier single-viewport requirement concerned four overview zones and should not be treated as a hard no-scroll rule for a card grid or a deep table. The design preserves persistent controls and permits scrolling in the main area and inside cards.

Default choices accepted on 2 November 2026

The second iteration was proposed with ten defaults; the user changed one (card layout is free-form rather than an ordered list) and accepted the rest: the platform’s categories replace the first-iteration sleeves; a category has its own target with products implementing it; sleeves are independent versioned copies, never links; a selection spanning categories may be saved as a sleeve and is marked mixed; sleeves are tagged from the fixed category list only; one shared repository with a browse, inspect, rename, edit-as-new-version and archive screen; the context bar holds members, scenario, basis, period and reference; the repository holds a fixed catalogue plus saved cards with settings; the fourteen-type starter catalogue, since joined by Active risk & return.

Reference screenshots should inform visual refinement only when actually available for inspection. This document does not claim to reproduce or critique any specific unseen Venn screen. See reference-material handling.

03 / Layout and navigation

The workspace is the unit of analysis.

The default screen opens directly on the workspace. A persistent context header identifies the workspace, save state, undo and redo, and the actions that create allocations and snapshots. Beneath it a comparison strip lists the compared allocations and marks the one being edited. Below that, the builder rail and the card area share an adjustable horizontal split. Two secondary screens exist: the workspace list (home) and the sleeve repository. The card repository is a sheet over the workspace, not a screen.

Context header and comparison strip

Intent: make the current analytical context explicit. Contains: workspace name (editable), active scenario or base case, autosave state, undo/redo, Add allocation, Snapshot, versions and activity, export, and lightweight navigation back to the workspace list. The strip shows each compared allocation with its colour, live/snapshot label and an editing marker, plus Add.

Behavior: a change of card, metric or row keeps these controls in place. “Remove from comparison” removes a view member; it never deletes a saved allocation. Selecting a strip member makes it the active allocation in the builder.

Every displayed comparison can be traced to a named allocation and version, a scenario or base case, and the current analysis period.

Builder rail

Intent: support precise edits while users inspect their effects. Contains: no brand heading. The header and tabs sit on navy; the panel below is white, so the tree reads like the cards beside it. A header naming the allocation being edited (a selector with its kind, basis and benchmark labels), then four tabs that each take the rail’s full height. Build: the category tree with inline weights, select mode for building sleeves, expand/collapse. Weights are typed only, with no stepper buttons: a typed value is marked pending and nothing recalculates until Enter applies it; Esc discards it and the arrow keys move between weights. Add: investment search with category filter chips; a chip alone browses its category. Sleeves: the repository grouped by category, each with Apply and Compare. Check: what needs attention (unallocated weight, categories at index level, products over target, limited histories, a missing benchmark), each with the action that fixes it, plus the weight summary and the opt-in short/leverage setting. A footer under every tab keeps the category bar and the allocated total in view.

Behavior: the Check tab carries a badge counting its items. Tabs follow the ARIA tab pattern (arrow keys, Home, End). Its width is adjustable and it collapses to a strip that keeps the four tab icons and the badge; each opens the rail on its tab. A numeric edit does not navigate away or open a separate construction screen.

The cards remain usable when the rail is resized; minimum panel widths prevent essential controls from disappearing. Switching tabs keeps the allocation, the selected row and any selection; a category’s Add a product here and Check’s Find products open Add filtered to that category.

Card area and context bar

Intent: compare the same portfolio structures through several analytical lenses at once. Contains: the context bar (basis, factor model, period, reference, horizon and band, scenario entry point, layout menu, Add card), a context line that states the effective comparison in words, and the card grid.

Behavior: the first visible card of a new workspace is the Drivers & Differences table. Additional detail is added as cards rather than displacing the builder.

A change in the context bar preserves compared allocations, expanded rows where applicable, and chart emphasis.

Scenario drawer

Intent: edit assumptions with results still in reach. Contains: scenario selection, applied-to portfolios, assumption level/type filters, editable matrix, correlation controls, override indicators, and reset actions.

Proposed An adjustable docked drawer reduces the card area width on a large display. A compact mode may overlay it when space is insufficient; the current scenario remains labelled when closed.

Opening and closing the drawer does not reset card settings, selection, or unsaved input.

Workspace list

Intent: find and resume work. Contains: workspaces with their compared allocations, owner and last edit; create workspace; the entry point to the sleeve repository; demo controls in the mockup.

Opening a workspace restores its allocations, cards, preferences and scenarios exactly as last saved.

Sleeve repository

Intent: manage reusable product subsets outside any one workspace. Contains: category filter with counts, archived filter, search, the sleeve list, and a detail pane with versions, products and weights, add to a workspace, rename, re-file and archive. See Sleeves.

Returning to the workspace from the repository restores the workspace unchanged.

Panel priority: keep workspace identity, the context bar, selected allocation/reference labels, and edit status visible. Allow horizontal scrolling inside a wide factor table and vertical scrolling inside deep hierarchies and inside cards. Do not shrink essential labels until they become ambiguous.
04 / Users and workflows

One workspace, different depths of investigation

Portfolio managers primarily need rapid construction, understandable trade-offs, and stable comparisons. Analysts and researchers need precise assumptions, look-through detail, multi-factor inspection, and inspectable methodology. Proposed They use the same interface; the distinction is how far they drill, rather than a separate role-specific product. Permissions may control access, but this document does not invent a permissions system.

PM · Build a new allocation

Story: As a portfolio manager, I want to start blank, from a template, or from an existing portfolio/import so that construction starts from the most useful context.

Flow: choose an origin → name the draft → set category targets → add products with universal search → edit weights → inspect live analytics → add references or alternatives.

Acceptance: all three origins are equally discoverable; the draft opens in the same workspace; a category target with no products is a valid, fully modelled allocation; the user sees the total and current data coverage without a blocking “complete setup” step.

PM · Modify an existing allocation

Story: As a portfolio manager, I want an editable working draft with immediate analytical feedback so that I can understand the effect of a weight change.

Flow: select the active draft → expand a category → edit a product weight or the category target → observe the index remainder and affected cells update → inspect the cards that follow the selected row.

Acceptance: the edit changes the active draft directly; autosave status is visible; the UI does not create an unsolicited scenario copy; a valid result is associated with the latest committed edit.

PM / analyst · Compare alternatives

Story: As a user, I want four to six allocations aligned in one table so that I can compare drivers and benchmark differences without mentally joining separate screens.

Flow: add live allocations or fixed snapshots → set basis and period in the context bar → inspect contributions → switch to differences → choose shared or individual references.

Acceptance: switching to differences preserves row context; every reference is named; unavailable values are not displayed as zero; factor selection is shared across all allocations.

PM · Reuse a sleeve

Story: As a portfolio manager, I want to save a category’s products as a sleeve and apply it in another allocation so that a proven implementation is reused without retyping weights.

Flow: open a category’s menu → Create sleeve from category → name and file it → in another allocation, Apply a sleeve on a category → choose the version and whether to replace the products or fill only the index remainder → inspect the preview → apply.

Acceptance: the sleeve’s weights sum to 100; the applied products carry a “from sleeve X vN” tag; editing the sleeve later never changes the allocation; the sleeve appears in the repository under the chosen category.

PM / analyst · Define a sleeve and inspect it

Story: As a user, I want to select a category or a set of products, save them as a sleeve and see that sleeve’s own analytics next to my portfolios, so that I can judge it before applying it anywhere.

Flow: Select → tick products (or open a category’s menu) → Save & compare → name and file the sleeve → the sleeve appears as a column in every card → later, edit it in the repository and show the new version in the column.

Acceptance: one action saves the sleeve and adds the column; the column reads “Sleeve v1” and is 100% allocated; the source allocation is unchanged apart from optional tags; the column never changes silently when the sleeve gets a new version.

Researcher · Test assumptions

Story: As a researcher, I want to change returns, variances, betas, and correlations at several levels so that I can inspect sensitivity to alternative assumptions.

Flow: open scenario drawer → select portfolio scope → find and edit matrix cells → inspect base/scenario results side by side → add a scenario comparison or projection card.

Acceptance: overridden values are identifiable; base values remain inspectable; out-of-scope allocations remain unchanged; invalid input is flagged precisely; scenario work autosaves to this workspace.

Analyst · Compose a view

Story: As an analyst, I want to assemble the cards that answer today’s question—drawdowns, stress periods, rolling volatility—and keep a configured card for next time.

Flow: Add card → choose from the catalogue or saved cards → place and size it → open its settings → save it to the repository with a name → remove the cards no longer needed.

Acceptance: every card obeys the context bar; a saved card reappears in another workspace with the same settings; adding or removing a card is undoable; the layout is remembered per workspace.

Analyst · Explain a driver

Story: As an analyst, I want to expand a category and inspect its products and index remainder so that I can explain a contribution without losing the parent context.

Flow: select a category row → expand into products and the index remainder → choose return, risk, weight, or factors → inspect the cards that follow the row → adjust legend emphasis.

Acceptance: the table retains a coherent hierarchy; parent totals are not double counted; a following card’s subtitle names the selected row, metric, unit, and relevant period.

Analyst · Understand a data limitation

Story: As an analyst, I want to see whether a value uses actual history, a backtest, a proxy, or incomplete coverage so that I can judge its suitability.

Flow: inspect a basis or coverage label → open context → identify affected products and periods → continue analysis using the available values.

Acceptance: limitations appear close to affected results; missingness and true zero are distinct; mixed bases are visible across comparison columns rather than hidden in a global footnote.

05 / Construction contract

A category tree with index-level modelling

The construction surface is a tree with three levels: the portfolio, its categories, and the products inside each category. The categories are the platform’s fixed list—Public Equity, Investment Grade Fixed Income, Other Fixed Income, Hedge Funds, Private Equity & Other Private Assets, Real Assets, Cash—each with a colour used consistently in every card. Users do not create, rename or reorder categories. Product rows identify their object type (fund, ETF, index, security, or other supported investment) and expose precise numeric weights. Universal search spans supported investment types and sleeves with clear labels so that similarly named objects are distinguishable.

Index-level modelling

Confirmed A category has its own target weight, editable inline. Its products implement part or all of the target. The unimplemented remainder is shown as an italic index row beneath the products (“Hedge Funds Index · unimplemented remainder”) and is modelled by that category’s index basket, declared per category by the platform.

Behavior: lowering a product moves the difference to index level; raising the target above the products does the same. Products exceeding the target are allowed and flagged over; the category menu offers Set target to products and adding a product offers Raise the category target as quick repairs. The category line states how much of the target is implemented (“4 products · 45.0% implemented”).

A category with a target and no products is a valid allocation modelled entirely at index level. The sum of products and index remainder always equals the category target unless the category is flagged over.

Inline editing and residuals

Trigger: user edits a weight. State: an input may temporarily contain an unfinished number; a committed numeric value updates the draft. Output: the allocated total (the sum over categories of max(target, products)) and the unallocated amount update, followed by eligible analytics.

Edge condition: targets need not sum to 100%. Do not automatically normalize other rows or block exploration. Unallocated weight is distinct from an index remainder: the remainder is modelled, unallocated weight earns nothing and carries no risk, and must not silently be labelled or priced as cash unless a cash product is held.

Editing a target from 40% to 35% leaves the five-percentage-point unallocated amount visible and does not rescale unrelated categories or products.

Short and leveraged allocations

Trigger: enable support for the active portfolio. Output: negative and leveraged weights become available, with gross exposure, net exposure, and borrowing shown as informational metrics.

Edge condition: do not impose risk limits, breach banners, recommended limits, or approval gates for leverage. Type, numeric-range, and model-input validation are distinct from risk controls and remain necessary where the analytical input cannot be interpreted. Without leverage enabled, an edit that would take the allocated total above 100% is rejected with the resulting total stated.

Once enabled, a valid short weight or net allocation above 100% is not rejected because of a risk policy. Information labels explain their basis without making a recommendation.

Weight semantics

Confirmed The editable weight at every level is the total-portfolio weight. A product’s share of its category is shown beside it, read-only, with its basis labelled (“44.4% of Public Equity”).

Proposed Editing a category target does not rescale its products; the difference goes to the index remainder. A rescale is an explicit action (apply a sleeve to fill the target).

A product’s displayed share includes its basis. No edit silently moves weight between categories.

Search and import

Search: result rows show the investment name, type, identifier and asset class; sleeves and portfolios appear with their own type labels. Adding a product routes it to the category of its asset class and asks for its weight; adding a sleeve opens the apply flow. Adding an item preserves the current cards.

Proposed Imports provide a compact preview for identifier matches, weight basis, units, dates, and unmatched rows. Retain incomplete entries for correction; do not silently drop them or assume they have zero weight. Imported category targets are the sums of their products unless the file states targets.

The user sees which rows were added, require mapping, or lack analytical coverage. The interface can explain why a new product is not yet reflected in a result.

Selection mode

Trigger: Select in the rail’s Build tab, Create from a selection in its Sleeves tab, or modifier-click on a product. State: checkboxes appear on products; a selection bar reports the count and the categories spanned, and offers Create sleeve and Clear.

Selection never changes weights. Leaving selection mode discards the selection, not any edit.

Try the mockup. Its calculation exists to demonstrate feedback and state transitions; production residual, financing, and derivatives semantics remain domain inputs to be finalized.

06 / Sleeves

A sleeve is a saved, versioned subset of a portfolio.

Confirmed A sleeve is a named set of products with weights that sum to 100, filed under one category, kept in a repository shared by every workspace. It is created from an existing allocation and either applied to a category of another (or the same) allocation, or compared on its own as a column of the workspace so that its analytics can be inspected. It is a copy both ways: creating a sleeve does not link the allocation to it, and applying a sleeve copies products in without a live link. Editing a sleeve creates a new version; earlier versions are never changed.

Create from a category

Trigger: Create sleeve from category in the category’s row menu. Output: the category’s products with weights rescaled pro rata to 100; the dialog shows the resulting weights, proposes a name, and files the sleeve under that category. On save the category shows a tag with the sleeve name and version.

A category of 5% and 3% yields a sleeve of 62.5 and 37.5. A category with no products cannot create a sleeve and says so.

Create from a selection

Trigger: Create sleeve on the selection bar. Output: the selected products rescaled pro rata to 100. The user chooses the category to file it under from the fixed list; when the selection spans more than one category the sleeve is marked mixed and the dialog says which categories it spans.

A mixed sleeve applies like any other: its products land in the category it is applied to, each keeping its own asset class. Tags come from the fixed category list only.

Apply a sleeve

Trigger: Apply a sleeve in a category’s menu, Apply on a sleeve in the rail’s Sleeves tab, Apply a sleeve on a Check item, or choosing a sleeve in investment search. Dialog: sleeve (tagged sleeves first, then the rest of the repository), version, and how: fill the category target: replace its products with the sleeve, or fill only the index remainder and keep the existing products. A preview table shows each product’s weight in the sleeve and its weight after applying, and the category total after.

Applied products are tagged “from sleeve X vN”. The category target does not change. Replacing is one undoable step.

Compare a sleeve on its own

Intent: define a sleeve, then look at its analytics before (or instead of) applying it. Triggers: Save & compare on the selection bar; Save as sleeve and compare in a category’s menu; Save and add to workspace in the create dialog; Compare a sleeve in the comparison strip’s Add menu; the “add it to the comparison instead” link in the apply dialog; Add to a workspace in the repository, which opens the chosen workspace with the sleeve added.

Output: a comparison column of kind Sleeve vN: the sleeve version fully invested at its own weights (100% allocated, every category implemented by its products), shown in every card next to the portfolios. In the rail it is read-only, lists only its categories, and offers Open in repository and Make an editable copy.

The column is pinned to its version. When the repository holds a newer version, the rail and the column’s menu say so and offer to show it, as one undo step. Adding the same version twice re-uses the existing column. A full comparison keeps the sleeve in the workspace without a column and says so.

The repository screen

Contains: category filter with counts, an Archived filter, search, the sleeve list (name, category, version, product count, author, mixed marker) and a detail pane: description, versions as selectable chips with their notes, the products with editable weights and a running total, Add to a workspace, Rename, Re-file, Archive/Restore.

Behavior: editing a weight starts a new version; a normalise helper rescales to 100; saving requires a total of 100. Archived sleeves disappear from the apply dialog and search but remain in the repository and keep their tags on allocations.

Saving an edit produces v(N+1) with the earlier versions intact. Allocations that applied v1 still show v1.

Open decisions Proposed Whether sleeves have owners and sharing rules; whether an allocation should offer “update to the latest version” for a tagged category (the mockup does not; it does for a compared sleeve column); whether a sleeve may hold index rows as well as products; whether a compared sleeve should default to a benchmark, such as its category’s index basket (the mockup assigns none).

Not to be confused: a compared sleeve shows the sleeve on its own at 100%. Its contribution inside a portfolio is shown by applying it to a category and reading that category’s rows.

07 / Drivers & Differences

One table with stable rows and explicit analytical lenses

The Drivers & Differences table is a card—usually the first and widest—whose rows are the categories, their products and their index remainders, plus unallocated and total rows. Keep the table structure stable while the user changes the question. The context bar supplies the time basis (historical versus forward-looking), the period or horizon, the reference and the scenario. The card’s own settings choose the metric (weight, return contribution, risk contribution, factor exposure), contributions versus differences, risk units and the shared factors. Factors have their own analytical dimension and must not be presented as children of every category.

Metric contracts

  • Allocation weight: total-portfolio basis. Differences are percentage-point differences, not relative percentage changes.
  • Return contribution: display contributions for the selected period or forecast horizon. Label annualized versus cumulative conventions.
  • Risk contribution: switch between share of total risk and contribution to portfolio volatility. Do not treat variance contribution and volatility contribution as interchangeable.
  • Factor exposure: use a shared factor picker. Each selected factor appears within every allocation group with its own declared unit.

A change of metric changes headings, formatting, tooltips, and the subtitles of following cards together; stale labels do not persist.

Reference modes

Shared reference: one benchmark or a compared portfolio supplies the reference for all comparison columns. Individual references: each allocation is evaluated against its own assigned benchmark.

State: the context bar and each relevant column header identify the effective reference. Switching modes preserves contributions and only changes the relative view.

Edge condition: if an allocation lacks an individual benchmark, show “No benchmark assigned” and unavailable differences—not zero or a silent fallback.

Changing the reference changes the difference calculation and its label together. A portfolio compared with itself yields zero where both values are available.

Hierarchy and different structures

Expansion reveals products and the index remainder of a category. Parent and child presentation must explain aggregation, residuals, and any non-additive measure. Because every allocation uses the same categories, rows align across allocations without a mapping step; a product absent from an allocation is zero only when absence is known, and unavailable data is marked separately.

Expanding a row does not shift columns out of alignment or duplicate a parent subtotal in a grand total. Expansion state is shared across allocations.

Factors and width management

The user chooses the same set of factors across all allocations. Factors appear together rather than one at a time. Sticky row labels and horizontally scrollable grouped headers preserve orientation as factor count increases.

Edge condition: factor units can differ. Do not imply that an equity beta and a rate sensitivity can be compared numerically without their definitions. Missing model exposure is “Unavailable,” not a neutral zero.

Adding a factor adds it to every allocation group. Removing it removes all corresponding columns while preserving selection of the other factors.

Row selection

Selecting a row makes it the workspace’s selected row. Cards that follow the selected row (cumulative return, factor exposures, projections, scenario comparison) retitle and refetch; cards that do not follow are unaffected. Find-a-row filters the table while keeping parent context.

Selecting a product row updates every following card’s subtitle and data together; a following card never presents the previous row’s series under the new title.

Table density

Confirmed Scenario pairing and factor groups never multiply each other (see Scenario columns). Expand all and collapse all act on every category. The card may be resized; its table scrolls inside the card with sticky headers and row labels.

At the card’s minimum size the row labels and the first allocation group remain visible.

Scenario columns Confirmed When a scenario is active, each in-scope allocation shows two columns: Scenario and Δ vs Base. A Scenario display control offers “Scenario + Δ vs Base” (the default) and “Base | Scenario” for users who want both raw values. Out-of-scope allocations keep a single column labelled with their own assumption set.

Density rule: scenario pairing and factor groups never multiply each other. With Factor exposure selected and a scenario active, each factor shows its Scenario value only; Δ vs Base and the base value appear in the cell tooltip and in the following cards. The maximum column count is therefore allocations × max(2, factors), not allocations × 2 × factors. Docking the scenario drawer must not hide the sticky row labels or the first allocation group.

What a cell means Confirmed The two comparisons are applied in a fixed order and never combined implicitly:

  1. Value is computed under the allocation’s effective assumption set (base or the active scenario).
  2. Contributions / Differences compares that value with the reference evaluated under the same assumption set. If the reference cannot be evaluated under the scenario, the difference is unavailable and says why. It never falls back to the base-case reference.
  3. Δ vs Base is the Scenario cell minus the Base cell of the same type. In Differences mode it is the change in the difference, in percentage points.

Each allocation header states its assumption set and reference on separate lines, for example “Strategic Core · Scenario: Rates +100bp · vs Global 60/40”. The Δ column header reads “Δ vs Base”, never “Difference”, so it cannot be confused with the reference difference.

08 / Historical analysis and cross-asset coherence

Comparable views without disguising different data bases

Cumulative return (growth of 100) leads the historical card set. The default period is the common usable history across compared allocations. Use actual portfolio history when complete position/weight history is available; otherwise backtest the current weights, with quarterly rebalancing as the default rule. Every comparison must show which basis it uses. Index remainders are backtested with their category’s index basket and disclosed as such.

Actual history versus current-weight backtest

Trigger: load or modify an allocation. Output: a basis label identifies Actual history or Current-weight backtest. A methodology detail states the period and rebalance convention.

Edge condition: “complete history” requires a defined completeness test. Partial positions, missing valuation periods, and incomplete transactions must not be silently presented as actual performance.

Confirmed When a user edits the weights of an allocation that has actual history, its historical values switch to a current-weight backtest of the edited draft, and the column badge changes to “Edited · current-weight backtest”. The actual track record stays available in the cumulative-return card as a separate, dashed “Actual history” series for that allocation. The table never mixes the two bases in one column. Undoing back to the unedited weights restores the Actual history basis.

A comparison containing actual and backtested columns visibly identifies both. A backtest does not appear as a realized track record.

Common inception and the period control

Default: start at the latest eligible inception among the compared allocations, using one common evaluation window. The period lives in the context bar and applies to every historical card.

Proposed Expose presets (common history, last 1, 3, 5, 10 years) and custom months. Explain a shortened range caused by a newly added allocation. Preserve the user’s selected range where possible, but report missing coverage rather than extrapolating.

Adding a short-history portfolio updates the displayed period and explains the change. Removing it does not silently alter an explicitly chosen range.

Unified multi-asset presentation

Use consistent labels and controls for weights, contribution, risk, factors, and references across major asset classes. Underlying differences in valuation frequency, proxy usage, currency treatment, model coverage, and lag remain visible through data context.

The interface must not imply daily observed accuracy for monthly or quarterly-valued investments. The common analytical framework does not authorize inventing correlations, values, or exposures for unsupported assets.

Illiquid and frequently valued assets can coexist in the view; their basis and freshness remain inspectable at row or portfolio level.

Coverage, units, and availability

Proposed Products without return history are excluded from historical figures and disclosed on the card; products whose history starts after the period start use their asset-class index as a proxy before inception and say so. Keep true zero, no exposure, stale data, partial coverage, and unavailable calculation as distinct states.

Open decisions: reporting currency and hedging convention; return net/gross-of-fee convention; observation frequency; historical factor coverage; annualization; handling of asynchronous valuations; portfolio-level benchmark inheritance into categories.

A numeric value is accompanied by enough context to understand its basis. A disabled card explains why it is unavailable and preserves the rest of the workspace.

09 / Assumption editing

A matrix for researchers, immediate results for everyone

The platform model provides the base assumptions. Users may temporarily override expected returns, variances or volatilities, betas, correlations, and other supported model inputs at asset-class, factor, and holding levels. The interface exposes these as scenario assumptions; it does not edit the underlying platform model. Scenarios remain local to the current workspace and autosave there. A scenario changes forward-looking figures only; historical cards are unaffected and say so.

Matrix editing

Trigger: open the drawer and select a level and assumption type. State: editable matrix cells show current scenario values, units, and overridden status. Output: a committed valid edit recalculates eligible scenario outputs for the chosen scope.

Proposed Support search, filtering to overridden cells, copy/paste, per-cell reset, grouped reset, and a complete scenario reset.

Editing a percentage in a cell cannot be mistaken for entering its decimal representation. Variance and volatility are clearly differentiated and cannot share an ambiguous “Risk” heading.

Correlation matrix plus pair editor

Use a full matrix to scan relationships and a focused pair editor to inspect or alter one relationship precisely. Selection is synchronized: selecting a matrix cell identifies the same pair in the editor.

Proposed Mirror changes across symmetric cells; make the diagonal non-editable where the model requires it. Show values and labels in addition to colour intensity.

Editing A–B updates B–A consistently. Out-of-range or nonnumeric values remain identifiable and do not produce seemingly valid refreshed analytics.

Scope and base comparison

Default application scope is all compared allocations; users can select a subset. Results show Scenario and Δ vs Base by default, with a Base | Scenario option (see Drivers & Differences, Scenario columns). Portfolio and scenario selection are separate state dimensions. The active scenario is named in the context header and in every forward-looking card’s subtitle.

Edge condition: an asset-level override may affect multiple holdings or portfolios through shared exposures. The effective scope must remain explicit. An allocation outside scope stays on its base or previously selected assumption set.

Changing scope never changes construction weights. Out-of-scope outputs do not animate as though they were recalculated under the new scenario.

Precedence and mathematical validity

Proposed Precedence is holding override → asset-class override → factor premium; show effective value, inherited value, source level, and active override where inputs overlap. Retain last valid results while invalid inputs are being corrected, with an unmistakable stale/invalid status. A correlation matrix that is not positive semidefinite makes scenario results unavailable, with a notice naming the scenario, and leaves base results untouched.

Open decisions: dependencies among beta, variance, and correlation edits. The UI must not silently repair a model input or substitute values.

Users can identify which value the scenario uses. A model validation failure identifies affected assumptions and does not overwrite the last valid base case.

The scenario example demonstrates the interaction with placeholder data. Multiple named scenarios, duplication, and version recovery are proposed extensions of the confirmed workspace-only storage choice—not previously confirmed preferences.

10 / The card workspace

The analysis is a collection of cards.

Confirmed The analytics area is a free-form grid of cards. Each card is one piece of analysis taken from a card repository: a fixed catalogue of card types plus cards a user has saved with their settings. The Drivers & Differences table is a card like any other, though it is the anchor of a new workspace. A card is never an unrelated dashboard widget: it obeys the context bar, it names its basis, period or horizon, unit and scenario in its subtitle, and the cards that support it follow the selected row of the table.

Layout

Grid: twelve columns, fixed row height, the card area scrolls vertically. Move: drag by the header, or arrow keys on a focused card. Resize: drag the corner handle, or Shift + arrow keys; each type declares a default and a minimum size. Collision: a dropped card pushes the cards it lands on down, then the grid compacts upward. The layout menu offers Pack cards into a tidy layout.

Proposed Moving and resizing are remembered per workspace but are not undo steps; adding, removing, duplicating and changing a card’s settings are.

No two cards overlap after any move or resize. A card can be moved and resized with the keyboard alone.

Card chrome

Header: drag handle, title, subtitle stating the effective context, settings (when the type has settings), and a menu: Follow the selected row (where supported), Show data table, Save to repository, Duplicate, Remove. Body: the visualisation or table; a data-table twin of every chart is available in the card.

States: loading keeps the previous content labelled updating; an unavailable result names the reason and offers Retry; a card whose basis does not apply (a historical card while the context is forward-looking) still renders on its own basis and says so.

Every card can be read without colour: its legend, headers and units are text. The data table of a chart carries the same values and labels as the chart.

Shared context versus card settings

Context bar (shared): basis, period (or custom months), reference mode and benchmark or portfolio, horizon and band, the active scenario, the compared allocations. Card settings (own): metric and values for the table; count of drawdowns; rolling measure; risk units; factors; projection basis; the metric of a scenario comparison; whether the card follows the selected row.

Rule: Basis applies to the cards that can show either history or the model (Drivers & Differences, Risk by category, Factor exposures). Historical cards always use the period; forward-looking cards always use the horizon and band.

Changing the period updates every historical card and no forward-looking card; changing a card’s metric updates that card only.

The card repository

Trigger: Add card in the context bar (or a). Contains: the catalogue grouped by Allocation, Performance, Risk and Forward-looking, each type with a description and its basis; saved cards with their names and settings, shared across workspaces, with Delete.

Behavior: adding places the card at the first free position of its default size and scrolls it into view. A saved card stores type, settings and name, never data; it reappears in any workspace with those settings and the host workspace’s context.

The same card type can be added more than once with different settings. Deleting a saved card does not remove cards already placed from it.

Chart cards

Every compared allocation is represented, in its fixed colour, with emphasis controlled through the legend; legend selection supports a set of allocations, not only one. Emphasis is shared by the cards of a workspace.

Proposed A shared crosshair reports aligned values at one date. Missing observations create disclosed gaps rather than invented interpolation. Preserve emphasis when the selected row or a card setting changes.

Selecting a row changes a following card’s subtitle and data together. Toggling a legend entry changes emphasis only.

Projection cards

Projection offers Standalone growth and Portfolio contribution. Standalone illustrates a common starting investment in the selected row; contribution reflects the row’s share of the starting portfolio.

Example: a 20% category with 30% cumulative growth is 100 → 130 standalone, or 20 → 26 within a portfolio initially worth 100. Labels must identify the starting basis and currency. Each emphasized allocation receives a separate small chart with matching axes so bands remain distinguishable; horizon and band come from the context bar.

Changing the projection basis updates the scale and labels while leaving the allocation and assumptions unchanged. Band labels identify probability coverage and horizon; a band is not presented as a guaranteed range.

Scenario comparison card

Shows the selected row’s result under the base case and each workspace scenario, with allocation and scenario identities distinct. The card reflects scope: not every scenario necessarily applies to every allocation.

Edge condition: too many scenario/allocation combinations require explicit selection or scrolling, not colour reuse that hides identity. Unavailable combinations remain labelled.

The user can map a plotted scenario value to the matching table result and assumption set. The card does not change the working scenario.

Factor models

Contains: two risk models, ISG (six macro factors at the asset-class level) and Axioma (ten factors: market, equity styles, rates level and slope, credit spread, the US dollar, commodities, inflation breakevens). Each has its own factors, loadings, factor covariance and specific risk. The platform assumptions stay the return model.

Switching: a Factor model switch in the context bar sets the model for every card that uses one: Factor exposures, the Factors metric of Drivers & Differences, the factor view of Risk by category, and Active risk & return. Such a card shows a model chip in its header: plain while it follows the workspace, filled with a pin when the card has its own model. The chip’s menu offers follow, pin to either model, Compare with the other model (adds a pinned copy beside the card) and the model’s description and coverage. The card settings carry the same choice.

Rules: the switch refetches only the cards that follow it. Subtitles name the model and its as-of date; the context line counts the cards on each model. A card never mixes two models’ factors: it keeps one factor pick per model, and with no pick for a model it maps the other model’s pick across where factors have a counterpart (Equity → Market; Foreign Currency → USD, opposite sign), saying so. A product a model does not cover takes its category index’s loadings and is flagged on the card and in the rail’s Check tab. Historical exposures are estimated against the model’s factors over the period. Factor-model risk does not change with a scenario, and cards say so.

Switching the workspace model leaves a pinned card unchanged and does not refetch cards that do not use a factor model. Compare with the other model is one undo step. Under each model, factor contributions plus specific equal total risk.

Active risk & return card

Answers: of each allocation’s return and risk, how much is its benchmark (passive) and how much comes from departing from it (active)?

Views: Summary, one row per compared allocation: return, passive, active; risk, passive, tracking error; information ratio; active share on the asset-class mix; and a line that reconciles the selected allocation (return = passive + active; risk² = passive² + TE² + 2·ρ·passive·TE). Risk sources: tracking error split by factor plus specific (from the factor model), or by category or product; contributions sum to the tracking error. Return sources: allocation and selection effects by category, which sum to the active return; a category the benchmark does not hold is benchmarked by its index; unallocated weight is its own line. Over time (historical basis): rolling 36-month tracking error and cumulative active return.

Measured against: the context bar’s Reference (default); each subject’s own benchmark (an allocation’s assigned benchmark, a category row’s index basket, a product’s stated benchmark, a sleeve’s category index); or a picked benchmark, product, allocation or sleeve version. Following the selected row, a reference portfolio is cut to the same row; where it holds nothing there, the category index stands in and the card says so.

Bases: forward-looking: active return from the platform assumptions (scenario where applied); tracking error from the factor model. Historical: realised from monthly active returns over the period; risk sources still come from the factor model and the card says so.

An allocation measured against itself shows zero tracking error and “— no active risk” for the information ratio. Unallocated weight counts as an active underweight and is noted. The three reconciliations hold to rounding under both models and both bases.

Adding a card type

Proposed A card type declares: id, title, description, group, default and minimum size, basis (historical, forward, both or none), whether it supports following the selected row, a settings schema, a data request against the analytics service, a renderer, a subtitle, and a data-table renderer. New types join the catalogue without changes to the grid, the repository or the context bar.

A card type that lacks a data table is still readable: its body uses text labels for every value it draws.

11 / Live behavior, persistence, and versions

Fast feedback requires trustworthy state.

Autosave and live updates remove explicit Save and Run steps from routine work. They also create obligations: users must know what is saved, which inputs produced a result, and whether the displayed output is current. A brief changed-cell highlight provides orientation after recalculation; it should not become a permanent before/after comparison mode.

Input and calculation lifecycle

Proposed Distinguish Editing → Saving/Updating → Current, with separate Error states. Commit parseable values at a stable edit boundary; avoid a disruptive recalculation for an incomplete keystroke such as a lone minus sign.

Keep the previous successful output visible while updating and label it accordingly. Results must be tied to the input revision that produced them; a slower older response must not replace a newer one. Each card requests its own data, so one failing card does not blank the others.

Rapidly making three edits cannot leave a card displaying the first edit’s result as current. The active input remains focused when values refresh.

Continuous save

Confirmed: drafts, cards and workspace scenarios autosave continuously. Proposed: visible states include Saving, Saved, and Not saved—retry available. Save failures are not disguised as successful persistence.

Open decision: persistence boundaries and multi-user conflict handling, including concurrent edits of a shared sleeve or saved card. The UI must not claim that a change is shared or remotely stored solely because local state has updated.

Leaving and reopening a successfully saved workspace restores its allocations, cards, layout and preferences. A failed save retains the working input and communicates the failure.

Undo and recovery

Proposed Provide undo/redo for construction edits, comparison membership, scenarios, and adding, removing or changing a card. Moving or resizing a card and display preferences are remembered but not undone. Version history (“Versions & activity”) records edits, sleeves created or applied, snapshots and scenarios, and enables restoration beyond a single session.

Edge condition: restoring a draft state should create a traceable new current state rather than erasing fixed snapshots.

Undo returns both the edited input and its analytical context to the intended prior state, then recalculates and autosaves.

Live allocations and snapshots

Comparisons support both. Live allocations reflect their current saved state; a snapshot retains the captured allocation/version, including category targets and sleeve tags. Their labels must remain distinguishable.

Proposed A snapshot is read-only and can be used to create a draft. Open decision: whether snapshot reproducibility also freezes model version, market data, assumption set, benchmark, and dates. Do not promise reproducible analytics from frozen weights alone.

Editing a live allocation does not change the stored weights of a snapshot. Any refreshed analytical basis on a snapshot is explicitly identified.

See the live-state example. Placeholder browser storage demonstrates the pattern; it is not a production persistence architecture.

12 / Interaction quality

A dense desktop interface must remain operable and readable.

Keyboard and focus

Proposed All controls, row expansion, numeric editing, factor selection, references, chart legend items, card menus and drawer controls are keyboard-operable. Cards are focusable; arrow keys move a focused card and Shift + arrows resize it. Shortcuts: / the rail’s Add tab with search focused, b collapse the rail, s scenario drawer, a add card, ? the list. Use predictable focus order and visible focus indicators. Opening a drawer or sheet moves focus intentionally; closing it returns focus to the trigger.

A user can edit a weight, expand a category, change a card’s metric, add and place a card, open a scenario, and close it using only the keyboard. Resizing has a keyboard-accessible alternative.

Tables and charts

Expose row and column headers, grouped allocation/factor headers, expanded state, and selected state semantically. Numeric alignment, consistent precision, and unit labels make comparisons readable. Colour supplements labels and icons rather than conveying the only distinction; category colours and allocation colours are fixed palettes, and status colours are reserved for status.

An assistive technology user can identify the allocation, scenario, factor, unit, and row for a value. Chart data remains available in the card’s data table.

Motion and change

Changed-cell highlighting is brief, subtle, and non-directional: a change is not automatically good or bad. Honour reduced-motion preferences. Announce completed updates without reading every changed numeric cell aloud.

Updates do not steal focus, scroll the card area unexpectedly, or animate continuously. Missing-data and input-error signals are distinguishable from changed-value highlights.

Responsive behavior and empty states

The primary target is a very large desktop monitor. Below about 1100px the rail collapses by default and reopens as an overlay; below 900px the cards stack in one column in their grid order and the context bar wraps. Empty, loading, unavailable, invalid, and failed states receive specific text and a relevant recovery action.

Open decision: whether tablet widths between 900 and 1100px should also stack cards; the mockup keeps the grid there.

At a narrower width, core actions remain reachable and headings are not cropped into ambiguous fragments. “No results” is not used to represent every failure state.

Quality review: verify a four-to-six-allocation comparison, categories with products and index remainders, a wide factor set, sleeve creation and application, a dozen cards with mixed bases, simultaneous scenario editing, interrupted saving, missing historical coverage, a short-enabled portfolio, and keyboard-only operation. These are product behavior checks, not a prescribed technology stack.
13 / Proposed delivery sequence

Protect the complete analytical loop first.

Proposed—not confirmed The following ordering is an implementation recommendation for review, not an agreed schedule. It preserves equal importance for creating, modifying, and comparing allocations. A feature’s priority reflects whether it is required for that loop to be trustworthy and usable, not whether its analytics are intellectually interesting.

P0 · Essential coherent workspace

  • Adjustable tabbed rail (Build, Add, Sleeves, Check) with the category tree, index-level modelling, inline weights, universal search, and blank/template/clone-import entry paths.
  • Live updates, continuous saving with honest status, incomplete allocations, and explicit short/leverage enablement.
  • The context bar; the card grid with move, resize, add and remove; the Drivers & Differences card with four metrics, contributions/differences, shared factors and reference modes; Allocations side by side and Cumulative return cards.
  • Four-to-six comparisons; historical basis labels, common inception, quarterly current-weight backtest convention, data/coverage states.
  • Scenario drawer with core assumption edits, selectable scope, hybrid correlation editing, and Scenario / Δ vs Base columns.
  • Create a sleeve from a category or a selection; compare it on its own; apply a sleeve; browse the repository.
  • Accessible controls and essential error recovery.

Why first: together these form a complete build → understand → compare → test → reuse loop. Removing a whole workflow would contradict the equal-priority brief.

P1 · Deeper investigation and reuse

  • The rest of the starter catalogue: drawdowns, underwater, summary statistics, calendar years, stress periods, rolling, risk by category, factor exposures, correlation, projections with bands, scenario comparison, active risk & return.
  • Two factor models (ISG, Axioma) behind one context-bar switch, with per-card pinning.
  • Saved cards in the repository; data-table twins; follow-the-selected-row.
  • Sleeve versions, edit-as-new-version, rename, re-file, archive.
  • Richer matrix productivity: bulk paste, override filters, inherited-value detail, and grouped resets; multiple named workspace scenarios and duplication.
  • Snapshot creation and restoration workflows with explicit reproducibility scope; deeper edit history.
  • Advanced search/sorting and comprehensive data provenance detail.

Why next: these improve depth and research speed after the core semantics are reliable. The full catalogue remains part of the target experience; deferral is a delivery proposal, not removal.

P2 · Polish and distribution

  • Exports of the visible analytical state with complete labels and context.
  • Extended layout preference restoration and advanced card customization.
  • Additional shortcuts and sophisticated multi-panel layout behavior.
  • Expanded edge-case import assistance and optional presentation refinements.

Why later: these should not delay a usable analytical loop. If external communication or import volume is a core release requirement, move the relevant item earlier.

Explicitly outside this front-end brief

  • Investment recommendations, trade proposals, or optimizer-driven advice.
  • Trading, order execution, approval workflows, or leverage risk limits.
  • A cross-workspace scenario library.
  • Designing the platform’s capital-market model, index baskets, data ingestion services, or forecasting algorithm.
  • Unrequested collaboration and entitlement architecture.

Front-end representation of calculation status, validation, units, provenance, and missing data remains in scope even where the underlying service is not.

Decisions to finalize before engineering treats semantics as fixed
  1. Analytics service: where the engine that takes arbitrary weights, category targets and scenario overrides comes from. The mockup’s data-access contract (workspace/README.md) lists every call the interface makes.
  2. Index baskets: who defines each category’s index basket, how it is versioned, and whether it is disclosed to users beyond the index row’s label.
  3. Weight basis and residuals: cash versus unallocated treatment, financing, and whether an index remainder may be negative when products exceed the target.
  4. Sleeve governance: ownership, sharing, archival policy, and whether tagged categories can be updated to a later version.
  5. Model metrics: exact return and risk contribution conventions, annualization, factor units, residual attribution, and historical availability.
  6. Override semantics: interdependent beta/variance/correlation inputs and invalid covariance handling without silent adjustment.
  7. Benchmark inheritance: which benchmark applies to a category or product and how references with different holdings are aligned.
  8. Snapshot and baseline scope: whether inputs, assumption versions, model versions, market data, benchmarks, and periods are frozen or refreshed.
  9. Multi-asset comparability: currency and hedging treatment, fees, valuation frequency, illiquid assets, proxies, and incomplete imports.
  10. Projection definition: horizon defaults, central statistic, confidence level, and weighted-growth treatment under rebalancing.
  11. Workspace persistence: private versus shared state, save conflicts, refresh behavior, and which preferences (layout, emphasis, rail width) are local to a user.
  12. Release scope: confirm or revise P0/P1/P2 after feasibility and user review. No schedule or effort estimate is implied.
