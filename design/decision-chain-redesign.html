Decision chain and benchmark setup: redesign recommendations

A review of the Decision chain card and of how a policy benchmark, an SAA and product benchmarks are set up. Every finding comes from the code in workspace/ and from a live run of the mockup; every figure in a mock-up is one the card shows today for the sample workspace.

Built 10 November 2026 Reviewed at commit 6a6c34a Samples: Strategic Core and Growth Tilt

Outcome. The redesign was built in the workspace on 10 November 2026. Decision 1 was answered with four names for the chain: Policy benchmark, SAA, Manager/Product benchmark and Manager/Product; each decision takes the name of the stage it arrives at, and the three together read All decisions. Decisions 2 to 6 took the defaults recommended below: one benchmark per allocation with a tagged exception; today’s category weights standing in for an SAA that is not set; SAA weights typed by category; both bases on Summary and one at a time on Breakdown; and Brinson effects headed From weights and From returns. The mock-ups on this page keep the working names used during the review, so where a label here differs from the workspace, the workspace is right.

Summary

Area 1 · Decision chain card

The card is organised by technique. Its reader arrives with three questions.

  • Did our decisions add value against the policy benchmark, and at what risk?
  • Which decision did it, and where inside that decision?
  • Has it been consistent over time?

Today the answers are spread over six tabs named Bridge, Levels, Steps, Lens, Reconcile and Over time, behind up to four rows of controls, 24 settings and the codes L0–L3 and S1–S3. The headline answer for Strategic Core, 0.32% a year behind policy, is not printed on the opening view: the reader subtracts 7.22 from 6.90.

Proposed: three views named for the three questions; decisions named in words; one sentence that states the result; one Break down by control in place of Lens and By; return and risk for the same row in the same table; the 30 reconciliation checks reduced to a status that speaks only when something fails.

Area 2 · Benchmark and SAA setup

Setup asks for a method. The user has three facts.

  • What is this portfolio measured against?
  • What is its long-term mix?
  • What is each product measured against?

Today an allocation carries two benchmark fields that can disagree (Strategic Core's rail reads vs Global 60/40 while its chain measures against Strategic Core Policy v2). The chain dialog is one long form labelled L0, L1 and L2, with five ways to supply an SAA, a default that raises its own warning, a 55-option list on every holding, and step order in the main flow. It is reached from nine places under five different labels.

Proposed: one benchmark unless the user says otherwise; a chain that works the moment a benchmark is chosen; one dialog that asks the three questions above and shows the result as it is filled in; one benchmark picker everywhere; SAA weights typed in place; product benchmarks that list only the exceptions; method choices moved to where method lives.

How to read this page

  • Findings are numbered C1–C20 (card) and B1–B12 (setup). Each says what happens today, where it comes from in the code, and why it costs the user something.
  • Proposals are numbered P1–P9 (card) and Q1–Q8 (setup) and list the findings they resolve. Mock-ups are drawn in HTML on the app's own surface.
  • The page ends with six decisions that are yours to make, the file and function each change lands in, and a suggested order of work.

Area 1

The Decision chain card

The problem, restated. The card holds a complete and correct attribution. What it lacks is a route through it. Its six views are named after the technique each one uses, its steps are named with codes, and its figures are labelled for someone who already knows which subtraction produced them. A reader who wants to know whether the SAA was worth departing from policy has to know that this is S1, that S1 lives under Steps, that the return is in one table and the risk in another, and that Selection in that table is not the step called Selection.

Why it matters. This is the card a CIO or an investment committee reads to judge decisions and the people who made them. If the card needs a guide, the conclusions are drawn by whoever is doing the guiding. A reader should be able to open it cold and say, within a few seconds, which decision helped, which hurt, by how much, and for what risk.

What gets in the way today

Finding your way

  1. C1

    Six views named after techniques

    Bridge, Levels, Steps, Lens, Reconcile, Over time. None of the names says which question it answers, so the reader opens each one to learn what it holds.

    ui/chaincard.js · VIEWS

  2. C2

    Steps and Lens are the same view

    The Lens tab renders through the same function as Steps, and the Lens control is available in both. Two tabs lead to one screen.

    ui/chaincard.js · stepsHtml(x, s, card, data, …) called for both

  3. C3

    Lens and By overlap, and one choice does nothing

    Lens offers Asset & allocation, Factor, Both, Matrix. By offers Category, Asset class, Product, Factor. Factor is in both. Choosing By = Factor only switches the Lens; the data request quietly falls back to category.

    Two controls whose states depend on each other have to be learned by trial.

    ui/chaincard.js · decompose: s.decompose === 'factor' ? 'category' : s.decompose

  4. C4

    Four rows of controls before the first figure

    Tabs, allocation chips, Basis and Step, Lens and By, then a heading. In Steps the table starts a quarter of the way down the card.

  5. C5

    Forward and historical side by side by default

    The opening view draws two return waterfalls, two equations, two risk waterfalls and two risk tables. Everything is half width and the eye has eight blocks to choose from.

    ui/chaincard.js · settings.basis default 'both'

  6. C6

    Reconcile has the weight of an answer

    It is one of six primary tabs. For the sample it lists 30 rows to three decimals, and all 30 read Ties.

    A check that matters only when it fails takes a sixth of the navigation when it passes.

  7. C7

    Workspace actions sit inside one view

    Levels in comparison: Off / Being edited / Every chain and Add the policy benchmark as a column are in the Levels toolbar. Both change other cards, not this view.

    ui/chaincard.js · S.setPref('showLevels', v)

  8. C8

    24 settings in one flat list

    Four of them (Lens, Attribute by, Step, Over time) repeat controls already on the card. Step order and Implementation step repeat the chain dialog. The one setting that explains the largest number on the card, Group specific by, is fifteenth.

    ui/chaincard.js · settings[] · ui/cards.js has no grouping key

  9. C9

    The way into a step is invisible

    Bridge bars open the step when selected, but nothing shows that they can be.

Reading the figures

  1. C10

    Codes where names should be, and several names for one step

    L0–L3, S1–S3, S2a and S2b are never spelled out. The first step is S1 · SAA on its chip, S1 · SAA decision in its heading, S1 SAA on the chart and S1 · Policy → SAA in settings. The third is S3 · Selection and S3 · Product / manager decision.

    chain.js · step label / short · ui/chaincard.js · settings.step options

  2. C11

    Selection means two things on one screen

    It is the short name of step S3 and also the Brinson effect column in every step's table. S1's table shows Selection −0.08 a few lines below a chip that reads S3 · Selection, which is −0.60.

  3. C12

    No statement of the result

    The subtitle is a method string: → Fwd and ◷ Hist · Policy → SAA → Product benchmarks → Portfolio · Strategic Core · % a year · linked (Cariño). The active return, −0.32, is not shown on Bridge at all.

    The reader does the arithmetic the card exists to do.

  4. C13

    The subject of the card is its smallest mark

    The return waterfall starts at zero with the policy's full 7.22%, so the three decisions (+0.21, +0.07, −0.60) are slivers at the end of two long bars.

    ui/chaincard.js · V.waterfall(el, { start: { value: lv[0] }, … })

  5. C14

    Risk is shown three ways at once

    A waterfall of contributions, then a table repeating them beside Stand-alone and Difference columns, then Cross term (TE², %²): a variance, +0.33, in a column of tracking errors. Difference is contribution less stand-alone and is not explained.

    ui/chaincard.js · riskTable

  6. C15

    Headers are abbreviations, and capitals change the Greek

    W POLICY, W SAA, R POLICY, R SAA carry no units. Table headers are upper-cased in CSS, which turns β into Β, so the factor table reads B PRODUCT BENCHMARKS and ΔB, and the optional x · σ · ρ columns read X / Σ / Ρ.

    css/components.css · .tbl thead th { text-transform: uppercase }

  7. C16

    Return and risk for the same rows are in two tables that do not match

    The return table is in category order with full names (Investment Grade Fixed Income). The risk table below is sorted by size with short names (IG Fixed Income). Its heading says contribution to the whole +0.67 and its column says Contribution, which sums to 1.00: two meanings three lines apart. For S3 by product the return table has 14 rows and the risk table 25, because each benchmark index gets its own row.

    The most useful reading, a large risk for a small return, needs the reader to match rows by hand. Real Assets is the case in point: −0.03 of return, 0.48 of S1's 1.00 of risk.

  8. C17

    Where the answer is not explained, the card shows least

    S3 by factor lists four factors worth +0.02, +0.01, +0.01 and +0.01, then one row: Specific (not explained by the model) −0.63. In the historical basis the largest risk row under S3 is Rebalancing and drift +0.97 of 1.88, explained only as Quarterly-rebalanced backtests less constant monthly weights.

    The step that costs the most is the one the card says least about, and half of its historical risk is not a manager decision at all.

  9. C18

    Levels mixes two kinds of measure

    Total return, volatility, Sharpe and VaR describe a portfolio. Active return of the step, Tracking error to the level before and Information ratio of the step describe a decision, and are filed under the lower portfolio with a dash for Policy. In Focus the card then prints differences of step measures between levels, which have no meaning.

    ui/chaincard.js · MEASURES · levelsHtml

  10. C19

    Over time cannot be tied back to the Bridge

    Its total column is cumulative percentage points (S3 −28.31). The Bridge says −1.05% a year for the same step and period. There is no row for the whole chain, so the years in which the portfolio beat policy have to be added up by eye.

  11. C20

    Matrix is wider than its content

    Eleven factor columns, most cells 0.00, followed by Row total and Asset lens columns that are nearly the same.

Proposed: a card that answers three questions in order

Drop the codes from the interface. Name each step as the decision it measures, and state the comparison underneath wherever the name first appears. Keep the Brinson terms, but as the second line of a column header, so that selection is never also the name of a step.

P3 · Summary: the result in a sentence, then the three decisions resolves C5 C9 C12 C13 C14 C18

  • A sentence first. Generated from the same data: sign and size of the whole, the tracking error, then the decisions in order of size. It replaces the method string in the subtitle, which moves to About these figures.
  • The chain as a strip. Four portfolios with their return, volatility and Sharpe; the decisions sit between the portfolios they compare. This is the Levels table and the bridge equation in one line, and it teaches the structure without a legend.
  • Bars drawn on the decisions. Return effects share one scale centred on zero, where zero is the policy benchmark. A 0.60 bar is now nine times a 0.07 bar instead of a sliver on a 7.22 bar.
  • One risk number per decision. The contribution, with its share. Stand-alone tracking error is one link away. The variance cross term leaves the view and stays in Checks, where it is a reconciliation item.
  • Forward beside historical only here. Summary compares the two because the comparison is the insight: managers were expected to cost 0.60 and have cost 1.05. Breakdown and Over time show one basis at a time.
  • Rows are the way in. Each decision row ends in a chevron and opens Breakdown on that decision. So do the strip's labels.
  • Portfolio measures and decision measures no longer share a table. Return, volatility, Sharpe and VaR belong to the four portfolios (Compare the four portfolios in full). Return effect, tracking error and information ratio belong to the decisions.

P4 · Breakdown: one control, one table resolves C2 C3 C4 C15 C16

  • Decision chips carry their value. The reader sees +0.21, +0.07 and −0.60 while choosing, so the largest decision is obvious before any click.
  • One Break down by. Category, Asset class, Product, Factor, Category × factor. It replaces Lens (four values) and By (four values) and every combination in it does something. Today's Both lens becomes a Split view item in the card menu.
  • Return and risk in the same row. The risk table disappears as a separate block; its one column joins the return table, in the same order, with the same names. The Real Assets row above is highlighted to show what this makes visible.
  • Sorted by size of effect, zero rows folded. A header click still re-sorts. 1 category with no effect opens in place.
  • Headers in words with units. Group headers say what the numbers are (Weight, %); column headers say which portfolio. No abbreviations.
  • Two control rows, not four. The allocation chips collapse to one menu here, since Summary is where allocations are compared.

P5 · Managers: one row per product, and the unexplained part opened up resolves C16 C17 C20

  • The benchmark is in the row. Each product is named with the index it is measured against, so −0.09 can be read as 6.0% of the portfolio in a fund expected to return 10.03 against 11.53.
  • One row per product in both columns. Today the risk table adds eleven rows for the benchmark indices, which is why it has 25 rows against the return table's 14. In the build, each product's risk should be reported net of its own benchmark; the engine can split an index's contribution among the products that use it, by weight. Until then the indices fold into one line, as drawn.
  • By factor, lead with the split. For this decision the factor view should open with two lines before any factor is listed: Explained by factor exposures +0.03 and Specific to the products −0.63, with risk of 0.06 and 1.12 of the 1.18. The second line opens to the products, largest first. Today that needs the setting Group specific by, fifteenth in the list; it becomes the default behaviour of the row.
  • Say what specific means where it appears. The part of each product's return against its benchmark that the factor model does not explain. For a fund this is mostly manager skill and fees.
  • Take Rebalancing and drift out of the decision's rows. In the historical basis it is 0.97 of this decision's 1.88 of risk and −0.01 of its return. It is not something a manager did. Show it as a tagged line under the total, NOT A DECISION, with one sentence: The difference between the portfolio as it was rebalanced each quarter and today's weights held constant. The Summary sentence should mention it whenever it is more than a quarter of a decision's risk.
  • Category × factor shows only columns that carry something. Factors whose column is zero throughout fold into 7 other factors, and the Asset lens column, which repeats the row total, goes.

P6 · Over time: the net line first, and totals that match the Summary resolves C19

  • A row for the whole chain. All decisions is the sum of the three rows, drawn as bars above them. Whether the portfolio beat policy in a year no longer has to be added up.
  • Two totals, both labelled. Whole period, points is the column the card has today. % a year is new and equals the Summary figure, so −28.31 and −1.05 are visibly the same result.
  • Colour by size. Cells are shaded in three steps each way, which makes 2015 to 2017 visible before any number is read.
  • The period switch sits in the view instead of in settings, since this is the only view it affects.

  • Units once, in the group header (Return, % a year), not in every cell and not left out.

Area 2

Benchmark and SAA setup

The problem, restated. A user setting this up knows three things: what the portfolio is measured against, what its long-term mix is, and what each product is measured against. The application asks for something else. It asks for an assigned benchmark in one dialog and a policy benchmark in another; it asks which of three kinds of object the SAA is before it asks what the SAA holds; it asks for a mapping rule, a step order and an implementation step. It asks in the vocabulary of the attribution (L0, L1, L2, S2) and it shows the consequences only after the dialog is closed and a card has been added.

Why it matters. Until the chain is set up, the Decision chain card and the chain step in seven other cards are empty. Setup is therefore the price of the whole feature, and it is paid once per allocation. If the first attempt produces a warning the user does not understand, or two benchmarks that disagree, the figures that follow are not trusted, and the people most likely to hit this are the ones seeing the tool for the first time.

What gets in the way today

Two benchmarks, many doors

  1. B1

    An allocation has two benchmarks, and they can disagree

    The assigned benchmark is set in one dialog and the chain's policy benchmark in another. Strategic Core's rail header reads vs Global 60/40 · Chain set, while its chain measures against Strategic Core Policy v2. Nothing on screen says there are two.

    The same allocation shows two different active returns depending on which card is read, and the user has no way to see why.

    allocation.benchmarkId · allocation.chain.policyBenchmarkId · ui/builder.js rail header

  2. B2

    Which benchmark a card uses is decided in four places

    The Reference menu offers Each allocation's own benchmark and Each allocation's policy benchmark as separate modes. Headline has its own Passive and active against, Active risk & return its own Active against with four options, Optimize its own Tracking error to.

    ui/benchmarks.js referenceMode · ui/activecard.js against · ui/optimizer.js

  3. B3

    Nine ways in, under five names

    The same setup is reached as Decision chain… (two menus and two Check items), Set up decision chain (a Check item and two card empty states), Chain set (a tag in the rail header that is also a button) and Review mapping. The benchmark half is Assign benchmark, somewhere else.

    ui/shell.js · ui/builder.js · ui/chaincard.js · ui/cardtypes.js

  4. B4

    The Check tab presents one job as two unrelated ones

    For Growth Tilt it lists No benchmark assigned as a warning with Assign benchmark, and further down, under For information, No decision chain with Set up decision chain. Doing the first does not complete the second: the chain dialog still proposes another portfolio as the SAA.

    ui/builder.js · check items · ui/chain.js · saaKind default

Inside the dialog

  1. B5

    One long form, written in the method's language

    The introduction reads The portfolios Growth Tilt's return and risk are decomposed through…. Sections are tagged L0, L1, L2. Hints refer to S2. Fifteen controls are on screen at once, before the two buttons at the foot.

  2. B6

    The defaults produce the dialog's own warning

    Opened for Growth Tilt, the dialog preselects another portfolio's custom policy (Strategic Core Policy v2) and another portfolio as the SAA (Strategic Core). It then warns, in amber, that The SAA holds products and lists fourteen fund names. Use the assigned benchmark is disabled because none is assigned, and it cannot be assigned from here.

    The first thing a new user reads is a four-line warning about a choice they did not make.

    ui/chain.js · X.open defaults when chain.js CH.blank() has no policy or SAA

  3. B7

    Five ways to supply an SAA, none of them type the weights

    Three radio options (an allocation, a library benchmark, derive) and two buttons (Create SAA from policy…, Create SAA from this allocation…). The buttons open the new-allocation dialog, create a full allocation in the workspace and say Adjust its weights in the rail after saving the chain. The one option that needs no work is explained as Each category's weight held in its index basket. The SAA then moves with the portfolio, and S2 is a pure benchmark step.

    An SAA is seven numbers. Entering them means leaving the dialog, finding the new allocation in the rail and editing it as if it were a portfolio.

  4. B8

    Three pickers for one library

    Assign benchmark has a good one: search, Presets and Custom, a mix bar and version on every row, Create custom benchmark…. The chain dialog uses plain drop-downs for the same nine benchmarks, with none of that. The library page is a third.

    ui/benchmarks.js assign dialog · ui/chain.js select elements

  5. B9

    Product benchmarks: the work is hidden and the list is not sorted by need

    The summary line is good (11 of 14 holdings use their stated benchmark · 2 the category index · 1 is an index) but the table is folded away. Opened, it lists all fourteen holdings in portfolio order, each with a drop-down of 55 options that cannot be searched. The three that need a decision are not first. Custom per holding is a third rule that means only the first rule, with changes.

  6. B10

    Method choices sit in the main flow

    Step order and Implementation step are choices about how to attribute, not about what the benchmarks are. Both have sensible defaults, both appear again in the card's settings, and here they come with a two-line caveat that every user has to read past.

  7. B11

    The preview shows mixes, not results

    The four tiles show a name and a colour bar. They do not show what the choice does: no return, no tracking error, no size of each step. The user finds out after saving.

  8. B12

    Saving leads nowhere, and every allocation starts again

    After Save chain the dialog closes and nothing else changes unless a Decision chain card is already on the grid. There is no way to apply one allocation's setup to another, though snapshots and clones inherit it: Growth Tilt starts from a blank while Strategic Core, beside it in the same workspace, is fully set up.

Proposed: three questions, answered where the result can be seen

Q1 · One benchmark, unless the user says otherwise resolves B1 B2

An allocation has a benchmark. It is what the rail shows after vs, what the Reference uses, and the first portfolio of the chain. The words assigned and policy stop appearing as two things.

The model already allows this: a chain whose policyBenchmarkId is empty uses the allocation's own benchmark, and the sample's Income Tilt is stored that way. The change is to make that the only path offered by default, and to treat a different attribution benchmark as the exception it is.

  • The exception is tagged. A user who reports against one benchmark and attributes against another turns on Attribute against a different benchmark in the dialog. The rail then reads vs Global 60/40 · attributed against Strategic Core Policy v2. Strategic Core in the sample is this case today, silently.
  • The Reference menu loses a mode. Each allocation's own benchmark and Each allocation's policy benchmark become Each allocation's benchmark. Headline's Passive and active against, Active risk & return's Active against and Optimize's Tracking error to lose the matching option. Each returns only in a workspace that has a tagged exception.

Q2 · A chain from the moment there is a benchmark resolves B4 B6

Once an allocation has a benchmark, every level of the chain has a sensible answer without asking: the policy is that benchmark; each product is measured against its stated benchmark, or its category's index if it has none; and until an SAA is entered, the long-term mix is taken to be the portfolio's own category weights held in index funds. The card fills in at once. For Growth Tilt against Global 70/30 that gives:

  • The card's empty state asks one thing. What is Growth Tilt measured against?, with the benchmark picker in place. Choosing one fills the card.
  • The assumption is tagged, not hidden. NOT SET on the SAA tile and one sentence beneath are the only reminders. They go when weights are entered.
  • Another portfolio is never proposed as the SAA. That default is what raises today's amber warning. If a user does choose an allocation that holds products, the warning stays but in one line: Strategic Core holds products, so Implementation will include their benchmarks. The fourteen names sit behind Show.

Q3 · One dialog: three questions and the answer resolves B5 B10 B11

  • Each row is a question in the user's words, with the term of art beneath it, its current answer, and one button. A row opens in place; only one is open at a time. L0, L1, L2 and S2 do not appear.
  • The result is in the dialog. The strip recomputes from the draft through the call the card already makes, so the user sees what a choice does to each decision before saving. It replaces the four mix tiles.
  • It can be saved as it opens. Every row has an answer already, so the dialog is a place to refine, not a form to complete.
  • Method is folded. Method ▸ at the foot holds Step order and Implementation step, closed, with their state in one line when opened. See Q7.

Q5 · The SAA is seven numbers: type them here resolves B7

  • One grid in place of five paths. The user types the targets beside the policy weights and sees the difference that the Strategic allocation decision will measure. The total must reach 100 before Save is enabled; the result strip updates on every edit.
  • Two starting points, one click each: the policy benchmark's weights, or the portfolio's weights today. These replace Create SAA from policy… and Create SAA from this allocation…, without creating an allocation.
  • The existing kinds stay, one step back. Use an allocation or a benchmark instead… opens the picker (Q4) with the workspace's allocations listed first.   • Leaving it empty is allowed and means what Q2 says. The tag NOT SET stays until the column differs from today's weights or the user confirms it.
  • Paste works. A column of seven numbers pasted from a spreadsheet fills the grid, as product returns can already be pasted elsewhere in the workspace.

Q6 · Product benchmarks: show the ones that need a decision resolves B9

  • Exceptions first, the rest folded. The dialog's existing count (11 of 14… · 2… · 1…) becomes the structure of the list.
  • A suggestion beside each exception. The index most used by other products in the same category is one click away: Listed Private Equity Index is what Oakhurst and Tamar are measured against, Broad Commodities Index what Bramley is.
  • A searchable field in place of the 55-option list. It is the picker from Q4, opening on indices in the product's own category, then all twelve indices, then benchmarks.
  • Two rules, not three. Stated benchmark, else category index is the default and needs no control. Use category indices for all 14 is a link. Custom per holding goes: changing a row is the custom.
  • Fix it once. A missing stated benchmark is a gap in the product directory. The tick box writes the choice there, so the next allocation holding Rivermead does not ask again.

Q7 · Method lives with method resolves B10

  • Step order and Implementation step leave the main flow. They sit behind Method ▸ at the foot of the dialog, closed, described in one line: Decisions in the order named · implementation as one decision.
  • The workspace's attribution conventions gain the same two entries as defaults for every chain, beside attribution model, interaction and linking where a method owner already looks. A chain stores a value only when it differs.
  • The two-line caveat about order becomes the definition shown on the control: The decisions always add up to the same total. Their individual sizes change with the order, because each is measured against a different starting portfolio.

Before anything is built

Decisions for you

Six choices shape the rest. Each has a recommendation, marked, and the alternatives that were weighed. Everything else on this page follows from them and can be built whichever way they go.

1 · What the three decisions are called

  • RecommendedStrategic allocation · Implementation · Managers. Each names who made the decision, which is how results are discussed in a review. Used in P1 to P6.
  • AlternativeAsset allocation · Benchmark choice · Selection. Closer to the attribution literature; selection then means two things on the card (the decision and the Brinson effect).
  • AlternativeKeep today's labels (SAA decision, Product benchmark decision, Product / manager decision) and remove only the codes S1 to S3.

2 · One benchmark per allocation, or two

  • RecommendedOne, with a tagged exception (Q1). Strategic Core keeps Global 60/40 for comparison and Strategic Core Policy v2 for attribution, and the rail says so.
  • AlternativeOne, no exception. Strategic Core's benchmark becomes Strategic Core Policy v2 everywhere. Simplest; it removes the ability to report against one benchmark and attribute against another.
  • AlternativeKeep two, but show both. No change to the model or the menus; the rail header names both. This fixes B1 only.

3 · What the card shows before an SAA is entered

  • RecommendedToday's category weights, tagged Not set (Q2). All three decisions show a figure at once. Strategic allocation then reports the whole difference in category weights from the policy benchmark, which is true but generous to the word strategic.
  • AlternativeThe policy benchmark, tagged Not set. Strategic allocation reads 0.00 and every difference in weights appears under Implementation. Stricter; the card looks half empty until weights are typed.
  • AlternativeNothing until it is set up, as today, with the shorter dialog from Q3.

4 · Where typed SAA weights are kept

  • RecommendedOn the allocation's chain, as a fourth kind beside allocation, benchmark and derived. They travel with snapshots and clones and add nothing to the rail or the library. Save to the benchmark library… stays as an explicit step for an SAA that several allocations share.
  • AlternativeAlways as a custom benchmark in the library. Versioned and shared from the start; every allocation's SAA then appears in every benchmark picker.
  • AlternativeAs an allocation, as today, but created and edited from the dialog and hidden from the rail. No change to the data; the workspace carries portfolios the user never sees.

5 · Forward-looking and historical together, or one at a time

  • RecommendedBoth on Summary, one on Breakdown. Summary has room for two columns per decision and the comparison is the point. Breakdown opens on forward-looking with a switch in the toolbar, which halves the width of every table.
  • AlternativeOne everywhere, switched in the card header. Narrowest; the comparison takes a click.
  • AlternativeBoth everywhere, as today.

6 · How the two Brinson effects are headed

  • RecommendedFrom weights and From returns, with allocation effect and selection effect beneath in small type and the formula one hover away (P8).
  • AlternativeThe standard terms only, with the same definition on hover. Familiar to a performance analyst; opaque to everyone else, and allocation already names the thing in the rail.

If the recommendations stand

A suggested order of work

Each step leaves the workspace in a state that could ship. The first two change no data and no layout, and remove the most confusion for the least work.

  1. Words

    P1 and the naming half of Q8: decision and level names, column headings, Benchmarks… in the menus, Check tab and empty states. Codes leave the screen.

  2. A benchmark is enough

    Q1 and Q2: the default chain, the tagged exception, the card's empty state. Growth Tilt's card fills on one choice.

  3. Three views and quieter settings

    P2, P3, P7 and P9: Summary with its sentence and strip, the Checks chip, grouped settings. The existing tables move under Breakdown unchanged.

  4. One table per decision

    P4, P5, P6 and P8: return and risk in the same rows, benchmarks named in product rows, Over time, definitions in place.

  5. The Benchmarks dialog

    Q3 to Q7: the picker as a component, three rows with the result strip, SAA weights, product benchmarks by exception, method folded.

  6. One door

    The rest of Q8: the rail header line, the single Check item, Copy from and Apply to. Then the workspace checks, their screenshots and the spec chapter on the decision chain.
