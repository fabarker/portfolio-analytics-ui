Rail, benchmarks and cards

Your feedback on the rail, benchmarks, the Basis toggle and workspace currency, turned into samples and proposals for review. Nothing here has been built: the mockup and spec are unchanged. Every sample uses the sample workspace’s allocations (Strategic Core, Income Tilt, Growth Tilt and the March snapshot). Figures are illustrative.

1 · Builder rail

Make products read as the leaves of a category

Today · Build tab

Why it fails today. In builder.js renderTree, a product row has no chevron, so its name starts about 6px left of its category’s name. The category colour band runs down the whole group, which makes the category and its products look like one block. The only cues left are bold versus regular type and the bold target, and the bold target looks like any other number.

What every sample keeps:

  • The faint silver line above each category.
  • Typed weights, applied with Enter.
  • Each product’s share of its category.
  • The index-level remainder as its own leaf.
  • Second lines only for exceptions.

What every sample adds:

  • Products are indented at least 22px beyond the category chevron.
  • One device ties the products to their category. It differs in each sample.
  • A + sits on every category row. Public Equity shows it in its hover state. In Real Assets it has been clicked: an empty row with a library search was added to fill the 3.0% the category models at index level.

Weights stay right-aligned in one column in every sample except 8, so the totals still scan top to bottom.

1b · Quick add

Add a product from the category row

The Add tab stays, for browsing across categories. Quick add is the route for when you already know where the product belongs, and you never leave the tree.

2 · Benchmarks

Presets, custom benchmarks and a benchmark library

Today a benchmark is chosen from six fixed entries (D.benchmarks), in two places. One is the “vs” select in the context bar (context.js). The other is the Assign benchmark dialog (dialogs.js assignBenchmark). The proposal keeps both entry points, but both show the same picker:

  • Presets are maintained by the platform team and are read-only.
  • Custom benchmarks are built by users and saved to a benchmark library, which works like the sleeve repository: versioned, shared, and archived instead of deleted.

  • Versioning, as for sleeves. Saving changes to a custom benchmark makes a new version. A workspace keeps the version it uses until someone chooses “Use v3”, so nobody’s figures change without notice. The picker shows “v3 available” next to an outdated one.
  • Presets can be copied, not edited. A preset’s menu has “Duplicate as custom”, which opens the builder pre-filled.
  • Fixed-rate rows (cash + x% a year) cover absolute-return targets. The cash rate follows the workspace’s reporting currency (section 4).
  • Checks before saving:
    • The total must be 100% (Save stays disabled until it is).
    • Each constituent can be used only once.
    • Products that use a proxy or have no history are tagged, as they are in the rail.
    • A benchmark can include another benchmark, but not itself.
  • Also as a column: a custom benchmark can be added as a fixed column from + Add in the comparison strip, as presets can be today (benchmarkAsMember).

3 · Cards without the Basis toggle

Every card has a fixed basis, and Headline shows both

The Basis toggle makes one switch decide what every card means, so a reader can’t compare an expected figure with a realised one without toggling back and forth. Without it, the basis belongs to the card: you add “Risk contribution · forward-looking”, “Risk contribution · historical”, or both side by side. The context bar keeps only the settings that cards share.

  • Basis leaves the bar. prefs.basis goes from the workspace.
  • Period applies to historical cards, and to Headline’s historical half. It shows whenever one is on the canvas, which is the current rule in needs().
  • Factor model, horizon and band keep their current rules: each shows only when a card reads it.
  • Scenarios already apply only to forward-looking figures. The tooltip says so, and historical cards ignore them.
  • Reporting in USD states the currency once, for every card (section 4). It is a button that opens the currency dialog.

How a card shows its basis

The basis is now part of what a card measures, so it opens the subtitle, ahead of the unit:

  • → Forward-looking is in accent blue.
  • ◷ Historical is in slate, followed by its period.

The glyph means the basis doesn’t rely on colour alone. Nothing else in the card header changes.

Four card types can read either basis: Drivers & differences, Risk contribution by category, Factor exposures, and Active risk & return. Each gets two catalogue entries that create the same card type with its basis fixed. The card menu offers “Show historical instead”. Drivers & differences is the exception: its Metric list is grouped into Forward-looking and Historical, so the basis comes with the metric you pick.

4 · Reporting currency

Choose a reporting currency when you create a workspace

Workspaces already store a currency, but it is always USD and nobody is asked. In shell.js, newWorkspace uses a one-field prompt. It becomes a dialog with a required currency and nothing preselected, so the choice is deliberate. Create stays disabled until a currency is chosen. If Create is pressed early, the error says which field is missing.

5 · For you to decide

Decisions, each with a default

  1. Which rail sample?Default 2 · colour spine. It keeps the category colour language you approved, adds the strongest grouping cue for the least ink, and survives at 320px. Sample 8’s separate weight columns can be added to it if you want the totals and product weights split.
  2. Is the + always visible or only on hover?Default always visible, quiet (grey outline, blue on hover). Adding products is the rail’s main job, so this is the one exception to “quiet until needed”.
  3. Where does quick-add search?Default this category first, with “Search all categories” one click away. A product from another category is filed under its own category, and a toast says so.
  4. What weight does a new row start with?Default the category’s index-level remainder when there is one, selected so you can type over it. Otherwise empty and required.
  5. What can a custom benchmark contain?Default indices, products, other benchmarks and one fixed rate (cash + x%). Allocations and sleeves are excluded: compare them as columns instead.
  6. How do custom benchmarks change over time?Default versioned like sleeves, shared with the team unless marked “Only me”. Presets are read-only and can be duplicated.
  7. How do cards that read either basis work?Default one card type, two catalogue entries, with the basis fixed when the card is added and switchable from the card menu. For Drivers & differences, the basis comes with the metric.
  8. Which Headline layout?Default A · grouped columns. It reads across like today’s Headline and keeps each basis’s method note on its own group header. B suits a half-width card; C suits many metrics and few allocations.
  9. Can the reporting currency change after creation?Default yes, from the header ⋮, with a confirmation, stated once in the context bar. Hedging policy waits for a later round.
  10. What happens to existing workspaces?Default they keep USD and lose the Basis setting. Cards that followed the shared basis take its last value as their own, so nothing on screen changes on first load.
