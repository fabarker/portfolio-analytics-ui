Ten ways to show drivers and differences

Drivers & Differences is the anchor card. It currently lays every allocation out as columns and every measure as a table. With four allocations and the eleven ISG factors that is 44 exposure cells before any contribution is shown, and it gets cumbersome quickly. These options keep the same data and change how it is arranged.

Every option shows the same five measures, for every compared allocation and against the reference (here Global 60/40):

By productReturn contribution
By productRisk contribution
By factorExposure (β)
By factorReturn contribution
By factorRisk contribution
Every measureValues, or differences against the reference

The mock-ups are live: the buttons work and every number comes from one small fictional model, so the totals reconcile across options. Product and factor return contributions both add up to the expected return, and product and factor risk contributions (with specific risk) both add up to the volatility.

01

One lens at a time

Closest to today's card. One measure is shown at a time, chosen from two short button groups (product measures and factor measures), with a bar in each cell. Rows are sorted by size, and only the largest eight are shown until the user asks for all of them.

Good for

  • Familiar: the existing table, with less on screen at once.
  • Sorting and the "show all" cut keep eleven factors manageable.

Costs

  • Only one measure is visible, so comparing return with risk needs a click.
  • Columns still grow with every allocation added.
02

Products and factors side by side

The card asks one question at a time (return, risk or exposure) and answers it twice: by product on the left and by factor on the right. Both halves add up to the same total, so the user sees the same return or risk explained two ways.

Good for

  • Directly connects "which funds" with "which risks".
  • Reconciliation is visible: both totals match.

Costs

  • Needs a wide card; two tables with four allocations each are dense.
  • Exposure has no product equivalent, so the left side shows weights.
03

Heat map of everything

All five measures at once, as a colour grid. The products block has return and risk columns; the factors block has exposure, return and risk. Each measure has one column per allocation. Colour carries the pattern; hovering a cell gives the exact value.

Good for

  • The whole picture in one view; outliers stand out.
  • Differences mode shows where each allocation departs from the reference.

Costs

  • Precise reading needs hover; numbers are small.
  • Twenty columns at four allocations; six allocations would need scrolling.
04

Focus allocation with differences

The user picks one allocation to focus on. Its values form the first column; every other allocation, and the reference, is shown only as its difference from the focus, with a bar that diverges either side of zero.

Good for

  • Answers "how is this one different" directly.
  • Fits the way a manager iterates on one allocation against alternatives.

Costs

  • The other allocations' own values are one click away, not visible.
  • Changing the focus changes every number, which can surprise.
05

Allocations as rows

The table is turned over: one row per allocation (and the reference), one column per product or factor. A composition bar at the start of each row shows how the total is built. Wide sets of factors scroll sideways with the allocation names fixed.

Good for

  • Adding an allocation adds a short row, not a wide column.
  • The composition bar gives a quick visual of each mix.

Costs

  • Eleven factors or ten products means sideways scrolling.
  • Long product names become column headers and must be shortened.
06

Small multiples

One small panel per allocation, each a bar chart of the chosen measure. Every panel uses the same order and scale, with the reference marked as a grey tick, so the eye compares shapes across panels.

Good for

  • Shapes are easier to compare than columns of numbers.
  • Reference ticks show over- and under-exposure at a glance.

Costs

  • Exact values need hover.
  • Five or six panels shrink each one.
07

Top drivers and biggest differences

A summary rather than a grid: the five largest drivers of each allocation for the chosen measure, then a list of the largest differences from the reference across all allocations. Everything else is folded into "Other".

Good for

  • Fastest to read; tells the story without scanning a grid.
  • Scales to many factors because it only shows the top ones.

Costs

  • Hides the small items, which an analyst may want.
  • Lists differ in order between allocations, so like-for-like reading is harder.
08

Product by factor matrix

For one allocation at a time: products down the side, factors across the top, and each cell shows how much that product adds to that factor. The row totals are the product contributions; the column totals are the factor exposures or contributions. All five measures come from one grid.

Good for

  • Shows why a factor exposure exists: which products create it.
  • The only option where product and factor views are literally one table.

Costs

  • One allocation at a time; comparison needs switching or a difference mode.
  • Large: ten products by twelve columns.
09

Return against risk

A scatter of risk contribution against return contribution: each product (or factor) is a point per allocation, and the points for the same item are joined so movement between allocations is visible. For factors, an exposure strip sits underneath.

Good for

  • Shows return and risk together: what earns its risk and what does not.
  • Lines between allocations show how each change moves an item.

Costs

  • Crowded with ten products and four allocations; labels overlap.
  • Less familiar than a table for many readers.
10

Dot plot rows

One row per product or factor, with each allocation as a coloured dot on a shared axis and the reference as a hollow diamond. Six allocations fit in the same row height as one, so the card never widens.

Good for

  • Scales to many allocations without adding columns.
  • Spread between dots shows how much the allocations disagree.

Costs

  • Values need hover; close dots overlap.
  • Depends on colour to tell allocations apart (legend and hover help).

Recommendation

Combine 01 as the default with 04 and 10 as alternative views inside the same card. The default stays a precise, sortable table with one measure at a time. "Differences" switches it to the focus-and-difference layout, which is the question managers ask most. "Dots" switches to the compact view when there are five or six allocations.

08, the product by factor matrix, is worth adding as its own card from the catalogue: it answers a different question (where a factor exposure comes from) and is too large to share space with the others.
