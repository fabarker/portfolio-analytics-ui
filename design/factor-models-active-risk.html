Factor Models and Active Risk

Two additions to the workspace. First, a way to switch every factor-based card between the ISG and Axioma factor models. Second, a new Active risk & return card that splits each allocation’s return and risk into the passive part (its benchmark) and the active part (how it departs from it). Front end only, on mock data behind PA.api, as with the rest of the mockup.

Status · built 2 November 2026

All four phases are built in the workspace mockup with the six default decisions below, and the spec is updated. One change from this plan: like Basis, the Factor model switch is a saved view setting rather than an undo step. Pinning, Compare and card settings are undo steps.

Summary

  1. One switch for the workspace, a pin for any card. A Factor model control in the context bar (ISG · Axioma) sets the model for every factor-based card. Any of those cards can pin its own model from a chip in its header, so ISG and Axioma can sit side by side.
  2. Every number names its model. Card subtitles and the context line say which model and as-of date they use, and a card never mixes two models’ factors.
  3. A new card: Active risk & return. For each compared allocation it shows total = passive + active for return and for risk, with tracking error, information ratio and active share. It then shows where the tracking error comes from (factor by factor, plus stock-specific) and where the active return comes from (allocation and selection by category).
  4. The benchmark is chosen, never assumed. By default the card follows the context bar’s Reference. It can instead use each subject’s own benchmark, or a picked benchmark, product, allocation or sleeve version. A category row is measured against its category index.
  5. Four phases, each ending with the mockup’s checks passing and the spec updated.
Part 1

Switching between factor models

The question is where the choice lives. There are four places it could go:

OptionHow it worksStrengthProblem
A · Context bar onlyOne switch; every factor card follows.Always consistent.Cannot put ISG and Axioma side by side.
B · Card settings onlyEach card picks its model.Flexible.Mixed models by accident; nothing says what the page as a whole uses; every new card needs a choice.
C · Workspace default, card can pin RecommendedThe context bar sets the model; cards follow unless pinned.Consistent by default, comparison when wanted. Works like Basis does today.Two places to set it, so each card must show clearly whether it follows or is pinned.
D · Model per allocationEach compared allocation analysed in its own model.None worth having.Mixes models inside one card; differences stop meaning anything.
Recommendation

Option C. Put a Factor model switch in the context bar beside Basis. Give every factor-based card a model chip in its header: plain when it follows the workspace, filled with a pin when the card has its own model. The chip’s menu has Compare with Axioma, which adds a pinned copy of the card beside it.

About this model and its coverage

Rules for the switch

  • Scope. The switch refetches only factor-based cards. Every other card is untouched and keeps saying which basis it uses, as with Basis today. Like Basis, it is a view setting that saves with the workspace; pinning a card and Compare are undo steps.
  • Default. New workspaces start on ISG. The choice is saved per workspace in prefs.factorModel.
  • Pinned cards ignore the workspace switch. Their chip is filled and shows a pin, and the subtitle ends with “pinned”. Follow the workspace unpins.
  • Compare with the other model adds a copy of the card pinned to the other model, placed beside the original on the grid. It is one undo step.
  • Subtitles and the context line name the model. A subtitle reads, for example, “β · Axioma factor model · as of 31 Oct 2026 · model exposure”. The context line reads “Factor model: ISG for 3 cards · 1 card pinned to Axioma”.
  • Factor lists belong to a model. Each card remembers its factor selection separately for each model. On a switch, factors with a counterpart carry over (Equity becomes Market); the rest fall back to the model’s defaults, and a one-line note says what was mapped. A card never shows two models’ factors together.
  • Coverage differs by model. Where a model does not cover a product (private funds are the likely gap), its exposures come from its category index and the row says “proxied by index in Axioma”. The rail’s Check tab adds “Axioma: 2 products proxied by their index” while the workspace uses Axioma.
  • Basis. Forward-looking uses the model’s own loadings. Historical estimates exposures by regressing the period’s returns on the selected model’s factor returns.
  • Saved cards in the card repository keep either “follow the workspace” or their pinned model.
  • On a phone the context bar wraps as it does now, and the chip stays in each card header.

Part 2

The Active risk & return card

The question it answers: of each allocation’s return and risk, how much is simply its benchmark (passive), and how much comes from departing from the benchmark (active)? It joins the card repository under Risk, as the fifteenth card type.

Mock-up · switch views and modelsIllustrative numbers

Active risk & return

Definitions the card uses

Active weights
a = w(portfolio) − w(benchmark), by product. An index-level remainder maps to its category index, so a category held at index level against an index-heavy benchmark adds almost no active weight.
Active return
R(portfolio) − R(benchmark). Forward: expected, from the platform assumptions. Historical: annualised portfolio less annualised benchmark over the period. The arithmetic difference is the default; geometric is a setting.
Active risk (tracking error)
The volatility of the active return. Forward: √(aᵀXFXᵀa + aᵀΔa) from the selected factor model, where the first term is the factor part and the second the specific part. Historical: the standard deviation of monthly active returns × √12.
Information ratio
active return ÷ tracking error. Shown as a dash, with the reason, when tracking error is zero.
Active share
½ Σ |a|, looking through to products and index baskets.
Passive and active risk
σ²(total) = σ²(benchmark) + TE² + 2·cov(benchmark, active). The card shows passive risk, active risk and their correlation, so the three numbers reconcile with the total.
Where active return comes from
By category. Allocation effect: (wP,c − wB,c)(RB,c − RB). Selection effect: wP,c(RP,c − RB,c). An index-level remainder has no selection effect by construction. A factor view of active return is a later option.

Decisions for you

Each has the default I would build with if you agree.

1. Which model is the default?
Proposed: ISG for new workspaces; existing workspaces start on ISG.
2. Should scenarios change factor-model risk?
Scenarios override the platform assumptions (returns, volatilities, correlations); the factor model is a separate risk model. Proposed: no. Factor-model risk ignores the scenario and says so, as historical cards do now; active return still uses the scenario.
3. Should other forward risk numbers move to the factor model?
Today Risk by category, Drivers & differences (Risk) and Projections use the platform assumptions. Proposed: keep them there for now, labelled with their source, and revisit once both models are in. Otherwise, two cards could show different total risk for the same allocation without saying why.
4. Arithmetic or geometric active return by default?
Proposed: arithmetic (portfolio minus benchmark), with geometric as a setting.
5. A category row’s default benchmark
Proposed: its category index basket, the same basket that models the index-level remainder.
6. Factor lists
I will mock a typical Axioma multi-asset set. If you have the real factor lists for ISG and Axioma, they replace the mock data without changing the design.
