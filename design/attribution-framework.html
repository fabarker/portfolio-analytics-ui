Decision chain attribution in the Portfolio Analytics Workspace

How the workspace should be extended so that an analyst can decompose a portfolio's return and risk along the chain policy benchmark → strategic asset allocation → product benchmarks → products, in asset space and in factor space, with every part reconciling to the whole. Written against Portfolio Analytics Workspace, product specification v3.0 draft (3 November 2026); chapter numbers refer to it.

0How to read this

Every recommendation is anchored to a named element of the specification: a chapter, a screen, a card, a control, a data object or a request shape. Where the specification already covers something, the existing element is cited and only the delta is described. Each item carries one of these marks:

Exists already in spec v3.0; reused unchanged Delta an existing element gains an option, state or field New a new element, placed in the existing information architecture Gap the spec lacks the concept, or names it differently Assumption data or calculation the spec does not confirm; listed in §8 for validation

Spec vocabulary is used throughout: allocation, category, holding, reference, basis (forward-looking or historical), member (of the comparison set), card, context bar, rail, factor model (ISG or Axioma), scenario, sleeve, snapshot, benchmark library. Where the framework uses a word the spec does not (manager, SAA, policy benchmark, decision level), §2 says which spec term it maps to and Appendix B lists every mapping. Request and response shapes are named as in the data access layer (TableRequest, ActiveRequest, ActiveResponse, Allocation, Benchmark).

Options are laid out for every major decision, with trade-offs and prerequisites. Where one option is recommended it is marked, but the choice is yours. Figures in examples are fictional.

1Summary and decisions

The specification already contains the two building blocks of a single attribution step: Active risk & return (§9) attributes one allocation's active return and tracking error against one reference by factor, category, asset class or product, on both bases; and the Reference control (§5) plus each product's own benchmark (§9, "Each subject's own benchmark") supply the two ends of a step. What it lacks is the chain: the system knows one portfolio and one reference at a time, has no way to name a policy benchmark, an SAA and a product-benchmark portfolio for the same allocation, and cannot show three steps that add up to the whole, nor the same steps re-expressed through the factor model with a tie-out.

The recommended shape is small in surface area and reuses the spec's patterns:

  1. A decision chain on the allocation New: three references per allocation (policy benchmark, SAA, product-benchmark mapping), set from the rail header menu beside Assign benchmark… and reported by the Check tab when missing (§4.1). The chain lives in the Allocation object next to benchmarkId.
  2. Derived levels as temporary members Delta: the SAA and the product-benchmark portfolio appear in the comparison strip and in every card exactly as "A category on its own" does today (§4, dashed member, tag, outside the six-member limit), switched on from the chain card (§4.5).
  3. One new card, Decision chain New, in the Summary group beside Headline: levels as columns, steps as a waterfall or table, a Lens switch (asset / factor / both) and a Reconcile view in which totals tie at every step (§4.3). It is built on an extended ActiveRequest rather than a new engine.
  4. A Step option on existing cards Delta: Active risk & return, Drivers & Differences, Return attribution, Rolling active return & tracking error, Factor exposures and Factors over time gain "a chain step" as a reference choice, so every existing analysis can be run on any one step without new chart types (§4.4).
  5. Method settings where the spec already puts conventions Delta: attribution model, interaction, linking, currency and cash treatment are card settings with workspace defaults under the header menu (§6), answering the §17 open question on metric conventions.

3.3Gaps, in one list

  1. No place to record an allocation's policy benchmark, SAA and product-benchmark mapping together (§17 already lists "Benchmark inheritance" as open).
  2. No derived product-benchmark portfolio (L2), and no way to show a derived level in cards.
  3. No ordered decomposition across steps, no cross-step interaction, no order sensitivity.
  4. No reconciliation view between the asset-space and factor-space attributions of the same step, nor across steps to the whole.
  5. No multi-period linking method, and no attribution over time (by month or year).
  6. No ex-post risk attribution beyond realised tracking error; no VaR or expected-shortfall attribution by step.
  7. No currency effect in asset space (the FX factor exists only in factor space); cash treatment is an open question (§17 "Residual treatment").
  8. Method conventions (Brinson variant, interaction, linking, fee basis) are an open question (§17 "Metric conventions") rather than controls.

4Recommended product shape

Five pieces, each placed in an existing part of the layout (§3 Layout: header, comparison strip, allocation rail, context bar, card canvas). Together they let an analyst define the chain once per allocation, see the levels as members, run the decomposition in one card, and run any existing card on any one step.

4.1Defining the chain New

The chain is a property of an allocation, like its assigned benchmark. It is stored on the Allocation (§7 data) as chain and is copied by Snapshot and Clone, so a snapshot's attribution is reproducible.

Where it is set: options

Recommended
A. Rail header menu, "Decision chain…"

Beside Assign benchmark… in the rail header's menu (§3 item 3; the menu that already holds select products, expand/collapse, rename, assign benchmark, snapshot). Opens a dialog with three rows: Policy benchmark, SAA, Product benchmarks.

Fits

The allocation being edited is the subject; the chain is as much a property of it as the benchmark is.

Trade-off

Three settings in one dialog; the comparison strip member menu (§5) should offer the same item for members not being edited.

B. Extend "Assign benchmark…"

The existing dialog grows from one picker to a stepped list: Policy, SAA, Assigned benchmark (the one Headline uses), Product benchmarks.

Fits

One place for every reference of the allocation.

Trade-off

Conflates the assigned benchmark (used by every card) with the chain; a user who only wants a benchmark sees four rows.

C. A fifth rail tab, "Chain"

Build, Optimize, Sleeves, Check, Chain: the tab lists the levels with their weights and a preview of the step totals.

Fits

Gives the chain a permanent home with room for the SAA tree.

Trade-off

A rail tab is a workspace-level commitment to the feature; the rail below 1100 px already collapses (§15). Better once the SAA itself is edited in place (§5.4 option "derived SAA").

D. Chain card empty state only

The Decision chain card (§4.3) opens with "No decision chain for Strategic Core" and the three pickers inline; nothing in the rail.

Fits

Detail on request (§2 principle).

Trade-off

The Check tab cannot report the missing chain unless the card is on the canvas; the setting is hard to find later.

Whichever option is chosen, the Check tab (§4) gains three items, each linking to the dialog: "No policy benchmark", "No SAA", and "n products without a stated benchmark (category index used)". The third is informational, not a warning, because the fallback is the spec's existing rule.

4.5Comparison strip and rail Delta

Derived levels become members using the rule the spec already has for A category on its own (§4): shown after the compared allocations with a dashed outline, a tag, a close button, outside the six-member limit, analysed by every card, inheriting the parent's scenario scope, never saved. Differences: they are switched on by the chain card ("Show levels in comparison") or from the member menu ("Show chain levels"), they persist while the workspace is open (a display preference, not undoable, §14), and their tag reads Level · SAA or Level · Product benchmarks. The policy benchmark can already be a member (§5, "A member is … a benchmark"), so L0 is added as an ordinary member when needed.

4.6Context bar Delta

Two deltas, both following the rule that a control appears only when a card uses it (§3 item 4):

  • Reference (vs) gains a fourth mode, Policy benchmark: each allocation is measured against its chain's policy benchmark. It behaves like Individual benchmarks (an allocation without a chain shows "No policy benchmark" and no differences). This lets Headline, Drivers & Differences and every active card measure against the policy without being set to a step.
  • Attribution conventions live in the header menu (§3 item 1, the menu that holds snapshots, versions, layout and reporting currency), not in the context bar: model, interaction, linking, currency, unallocated-weight treatment, and fee basis. They are workspace defaults; a card may depart from them and then states the departure in its subtitle, as the factor model does today (§7 "A factor model or scenario is named only where the card departs from the context bar").

Not recommended: a "Chain" switch in the context bar. The chain is per allocation, so a bar-level switch would have no single meaning across members.

5Modelling and analytics options

For each decision: the options, what the UI must expose for each (mapped to a spec element or marked new), prerequisites, and when each is the better choice. Nothing here changes what the UI calculates: by the spec's principle the UI "performs no financial calculation of its own" (§2), so every option is a request parameter the service honours and a label the card states.

5.1Return attribution methodology

Attribution model (asset space, by category or asset class)

Recommended default
Brinson–Fachler

Allocation = (wP − wB)(RBc − RB); selection = wP(RPc − RBc). The mock engine's brinson does this today, with interaction folded into selection.

UI

Attribution model setting on the chain card and on Return attribution; stated in About these figures. New

When better

When over- and underweights should be judged against the benchmark's total, so that overweighting a group that beat the benchmark is positive regardless of the group's absolute return. The usual choice for multi-asset.

Brinson–Hood–Beebower

Allocation = (wP − wB) RBc. Totals are identical; the split between groups differs.

UI

Same setting. The card must note that group-level allocation effects are not comparable across the two models.

When better

When the policy committee reads "allocation" as the raw return earned by weighting a group, or an external report uses BHB.

Interaction treatment

(wP − wB)(RPc − RBc): folded into selection (today), into allocation, or shown as its own row.

UI

Interaction setting; a third effect column and waterfall bar when separate. New

When better

Separate when a step mixes large weight and return differences (typically S1) and the analyst must not over-credit selection; folded when the audience expects two effects.

Product level and factor level

By product the step is each holding's (wlower − wupper)(r − Rupper), as the engine's product attribution; for S3, where the weights are equal by construction, it reduces to w (rproduct − rbenchmark), the product's own excess. By factor the step is Σf Δβf × premiumf (forward) or × realised factor returnf (historical), plus specific, plus cash where the levels' cash differs, as Active risk & return's "By factor" (§9). No new option; the factor-return source is §5.3.

Conventions a service must keep (additions to the README list)

  • Derived levels are evaluated from the chain, never stored; the same chain and revision always yield the same holdings.
  • Steps sum to the whole on the arithmetic reading within the stated tolerance; the service returns the ties, not the UI.
  • A level that cannot be formed (no SAA, benchmark not found, holding without any resolvable benchmark) returns a reason for the affected steps and the others still compute.
  • Scenario scope and the factor-model note propagate from the parent allocation to its levels.

10Open questions for §17

  1. Chain ownership. Is the chain a property of the allocation (recommended) or of the workspace (one policy and one SAA for every allocation)? A workspace-level chain is simpler but cannot compare two portfolios with different policies.
  2. SAA as allocation or benchmark. Should an SAA be editable in the rail (allocation) or governed like a benchmark (library, versions, read-only)? Both are offered; the house process decides.
  3. Benchmark inheritance (existing question). The product-benchmark mapping gives it a concrete form: stated benchmark, category index or override. Confirm the fallback order.
  4. Metric conventions (existing question). Confirm the defaults proposed in §1: Brinson–Fachler, interaction in selection, arithmetic, Cariño, currency in selection, unallocated at zero return.
  5. Residual treatment (existing question). Whether "treat as cash" should be offered at all in the chain, since it changes every card's convention.
  6. Snapshot reproducibility (existing question). Whether a snapshot fixes the chain's resolved levels (holdings of L1 and L2 as of the snapshot) in addition to the chain's identifiers.
  7. Levels in the comparison strip. Whether derived levels should ever be saved (as snapshots) or always remain temporary.
  8. Attribution on the forward basis. Whether a forward-looking "return attribution" (expected active return by step) is wanted by the committee, or only risk; the design supports both.
