Ten layouts for the Headline card

The Headline card becomes the summary of every workspace: one of only two cards a new workspace opens with, beside Allocations side by side. It carries fourteen measures on two bases for every compared allocation.

ReturnTotal return · Excess return = Passive excess + Active excess
RiskRisk (volatility) = Passive risk + Active risk · Tracking error
RatiosSharpe ratio · Information ratio
99% value at risk1 day · 1 week · 1 month · 1 year
BasesForward-looking (factor model) and historical (the period)

Definitions used in every option

  • Excess return is total return minus cash. Passive excess is the benchmark's excess return; active excess is the allocation's return minus the benchmark's. The two add up to the excess return.
  • Risk is volatility. Passive risk is the allocation's covariance with its benchmark divided by its volatility; active risk is the covariance with its active return divided by its volatility. The two add up to the volatility. Active risk is negative when departing from the benchmark lowers risk (Income Tilt below).
  • Tracking error is the volatility of the active return. It is a different number from active risk, so it is always shown.
  • Information ratio is active excess return divided by tracking error. Sharpe ratio is excess return divided by risk.
  • 99% value at risk is the loss exceeded with 1% probability over the horizon (1 day = 1/252 of a year, 1 week = 5/252, 1 month = 21/252), from the mean and volatility on each basis.
  • Forward-looking returns come from the platform assumptions; forward risk comes from the factor model (ISG here). Historical figures are realised over Jan 2015 – Oct 2026.

The figures are the mockup's own, for the sample workspace measured against Global 60/40, and they reconcile on both bases.

01

Measures as rows, bases side by side

Measures run down the side in four groups; each allocation has a Forward and a Historical column. Every number is visible at once, and the card widens only with the number of allocations.

Good for

  • All 14 measures on both bases with no switching.
  • The passive and active parts sit indented under the total they add up to.

Costs

  • A tall card: about 19 rows.
  • Six allocations means twelve value columns.
02

Allocations as rows, one basis at a time

Today's layout extended: one row per allocation and the measures across, in four column groups. A switch chooses Forward-looking or Historical.

Good for

  • Familiar and compact in height; extra allocations add short rows.
  • Comparing one measure down the allocations is direct.

Costs

  • Sixteen columns is wide; it scrolls in a half-width card.
  • Only one basis is visible at a time.
03

Allocations as rows, both bases in each cell

As option 2, but each cell holds both values: forward-looking above, historical below in grey. No switch is needed.

Good for

  • Both bases visible without doubling the columns.
  • Forward and historical can be read against each other in one place.

Costs

  • Dense: two numbers per cell across sixteen columns.
  • Sorting by a column is ambiguous.
04

One tile per allocation

A tile per allocation with total return and risk in large type, the splits and ratios beneath, and value at risk as a strip of four horizons. A switch chooses the basis.

Good for

  • The most legible summary; reads well on a phone.
  • Each allocation is self-contained.

Costs

  • Comparing a measure across allocations means scanning across tiles.
  • One basis at a time.
05

Decomposition bars

Per allocation, one bar shows excess return built from passive and active, and another shows risk built from passive and active risk with tracking error marked. Ratios and value at risk sit to the right as text.

Good for

  • Makes the "subsets" visible: how much of return and risk is the benchmark.
  • Negative active parts stand out.

Costs

  • Exact values need the labels or hover.
  • One basis at a time.
06

Forward against historical, one allocation

A butterfly for one allocation at a time: forward-looking values on the left, historical on the right, measure names in the middle, with bars drawn outward from the centre. Tabs choose the allocation.

Good for

  • Shows directly whether expectations match the record.
  • Narrow enough for a half-width card.

Costs

  • One allocation at a time; comparison needs the tabs.
  • Bars across mixed units (%, ratio) need care.
07

Tabs by section

Tabs for Return, Risk, Ratios and Value at risk. Each tab is a small table: allocations as rows, with Forward and Historical columns for each measure in the section.

Good for

  • Short tables that fit any card size.
  • Both bases visible within a section.

Costs

  • The headline is split: return and risk are never on screen together.
  • A click to see each section.
08

Focus allocation with differences

As option 1, but one allocation is the focus: its values come first, and every other allocation is shown as its difference from the focus, matching the Focus view of Drivers & Differences.

Good for

  • Shows at once how each alternative changes return, risk and value at risk.
  • Consistent with the Drivers & Differences focus view.

Costs

  • The other allocations' own values are one click away.
  • Differences of ratios read less naturally than differences of returns.
09

Table with a value-at-risk chart

Return, risk and ratios in a compact table on the left; on the right, 99% value at risk drawn against horizon for every allocation, so the term structure of loss is visible. A switch chooses the basis.

Good for

  • The four value-at-risk horizons read as one shape rather than four numbers.
  • The table stays short.

Costs

  • The chart is dominated by the one-year value; short horizons sit close together.
  • One basis at a time.
10

Statement lines

Each allocation is summarised in two lines of labelled figures, one per basis, written in the order the measures build up: return, its split, risk, its split, ratios, value at risk.

Good for

  • Very compact; fits a short, full-width card.
  • The additive relationships are written out.

Costs

  • Hard to compare one measure across allocations.
  • Less scannable than a table.

Recommendation

01, with 08 as an alternative view inside the card (the same Table / Focus switch as Drivers & Differences). Option 01 is the only layout that shows every measure on both bases for every allocation without a click, and the indented splits make the additive structure clear. The mockup's Headline card has been built this way for now, so the measures can be tried; it is straightforward to change once a layout is chosen.
