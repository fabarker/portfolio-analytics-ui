01 / Product intent

Make portfolio exploration immediate, legible, and comparable.

This platform helps portfolio managers, investment analysts, and researchers build and test new and existing multi-asset allocations. Its job is to expose historical and forward-looking return, risk, contributions, and exposures so that users can understand what drives an allocation and how it differs from its alternatives and benchmarks. Decisions remain with the user: the interface surfaces analytics, not investment recommendations or proposed trades.

Building from scratch, modifying an existing allocation, and comparing alternatives have equal product priority. They therefore share one persistent workspace rather than separate sequential workflows. The critical design test is whether a feature shortens the path from a question to a defensible comparison. Analytical depth should be reached through explicit controls, expansion, and selection—not by filling the initial view with every available dimension.

This document is a front-end specification with interactive examples. It defines visible behavior, interaction, state, and acceptance criteria. Numerical examples are fabricated demonstration data; they do not define production financial models or promise the accuracy of forecasts. Back-end model construction is outside scope, but model-provided units, status, provenance, and coverage must be represented faithfully.

One place to iterate

Edit a nested allocation on the left; inspect the integrated Drivers & Differences table on the right; explore the selected row below it. The scenario drawer allows assumption editing alongside results.

Success: a user can edit a weight, inspect the changed contribution, and compare it with a benchmark without leaving the workspace.

Consistency before density

One column per allocation supports comparison across four to six alternatives. Switches change the analytical lens while keeping the portfolio context stable. Factor exposure is an intentional exception: shared selected factors create grouped columns within each allocation.

Success: a user can explain what a displayed number means, its unit, period, reference, and scenario without opening another screen.

Depth through deliberate action

Expandable sleeves, a common factor picker, metric controls, and a chart below the table expose detail on request. The large-desktop target permits information density, but does not remove the need for clear hierarchy.

Success: expanding a sleeve or changing the selected metric does not reset comparison membership or force the user to rebuild their context.

Evidence, not advice

Differences, concentrations, contribution totals, and uncertainty are analytical outputs. The interface may describe them factually; it must not rank allocations as “best,” suggest a trade, or offer a recommended rebalance.

Success: every insight links to an inspectable value or view; no “Apply recommendation” action is present.

Read the workspace example as the primary product surface. The smaller examples below isolate interactions; they are not additional mandatory screens.

02 / Decision record

Confirmed direction and the status of this specification

Confirmed means the user explicitly selected that behavior during the interview. Proposed means a predicted preference or a necessary implementation detail that has not been independently confirmed. The instruction to develop this document authorizes a complete design proposal; it does not silently convert every prediction into a confirmed requirement.

Confirmed foundation

  • Single workspace; adjustable builder/analytics split; very large desktop monitor as primary target.
  • Direct working drafts with continuous autosave and live recalculation.
  • Nested portfolios presented as expandable sleeves; inline numeric editing; incomplete allocations allowed.
  • Shorts and leverage are opt-in per portfolio. Once enabled, there are no leverage risk limits; exposure and borrowing are informational.
  • Blank, template, and clone/import creation choices; universal investment search.
  • A coherent multi-asset framework, with historical and forward-looking analytics given equal access.

Confirmed analytical core

  • Drivers and Differences together in an integrated table.
  • Four to six allocations; live portfolios and fixed snapshots; shared and portfolio-specific benchmarks.
  • Contributions/Differences and Historical/Forward-looking switches.
  • Expandable sleeve rows; weight, return contribution, risk contribution, and factor exposure available.
  • Several factors together, chosen once and shared across all allocations.
  • Switchable reference mode; risk displayed as share of total risk or contribution to volatility.

Confirmed scenario and chart behavior

  • Editable capital-market assumptions at asset-class, factor, and holding levels in a scenario drawer.
  • Spreadsheet-style assumptions matrix and hybrid correlation editing.
  • Base and scenario results side by side, with selectable scope defaulting to all compared allocations.
  • Selected-row chart below the table; all allocations remain visible, with a selected set emphasized through the chart legend.
  • Forward-looking Projection/Scenarios switch; Standalone growth/Portfolio contribution switch.
  • Central projection and bands, shown in separate small charts for emphasized allocations.
  • Scenarios autosave within the workspace; no cross-workspace scenario library.

Superseded or interpreted choices

The earlier four-zone overview—Attractiveness, Drivers, Differences, and Actions—is superseded by the single Drivers & Differences screen. Recommendations and specific change proposals are explicitly excluded.

The earlier single-viewport requirement concerned four overview zones and should not be treated as a hard no-scroll rule for a deep table. The design preserves persistent controls and permits table scrolling.

“Canvas table” was interpreted during the interview as canvas tabs. This document retains a persistent analytics canvas with lightweight tabs where needed, rather than inventing a separate mode for every metric.

Reference screenshots should inform visual refinement only when actually available for inspection. This document does not claim to reproduce or critique any specific unseen Venn screen. See reference-material handling.

03 / Layout and navigation

The workspace is the unit of analysis.

The default screen opens directly on the combined Drivers & Differences view. A persistent context header identifies the workspace, active draft, save state, comparison members, and scenario status. Below it, the builder and analytics canvas share an adjustable horizontal split. Selecting another draft changes what can be edited without discarding the current analysis.

Context header

Intent: make the current analytical context explicit. Contains: workspace name, active editable allocation, comparison membership, live/snapshot labels, autosave state, scenario entry point, and lightweight navigation.

Behavior: a change of metric or row keeps these controls in place. “Remove from comparison” removes a view member; it never deletes a saved allocation.

Every displayed comparison can be traced to a named allocation and version, a scenario or base case, and the current analysis period.

Allocation builder

Intent: support precise edits while users inspect their effects. Contains: expandable sleeve tree, inline weights, total and residual state, instrument search, active allocation selector, and opt-in short/leverage setting.

Behavior: its width is adjustable and collapsible. A numeric edit does not navigate away or open a separate construction screen.

The table remains usable when the builder is resized; minimum panel widths prevent essential controls from disappearing.

Analytics canvas

Intent: compare the same portfolio structures through several analytical lenses. Contains: historical/forward-looking control, metric picker, contribution/difference control, reference selection, date or forecast horizon, factor picker when relevant, integrated table, and selected-row chart.

Behavior: the first visible data view is the table. Additional detail uses the canvas rather than displacing the builder.

A view change preserves compared allocations, expanded rows where applicable, and chart emphasis.

Scenario drawer

Intent: edit assumptions with results still in reach. Contains: scenario selection, applied-to portfolios, assumption level/type filters, editable matrix, correlation controls, override indicators, and reset actions.

Proposed An adjustable docked drawer reduces the canvas width on a large display. A compact mode may overlay it when space is insufficient; the current scenario remains labelled when closed.

Opening and closing the drawer does not reset table settings, selection, or unsaved input.

Panel priority: keep workspace identity, metric controls, selected allocation/reference labels, and edit status visible. Allow horizontal scrolling inside a wide factor table and vertical scrolling inside deep hierarchies. Do not shrink essential labels until they become ambiguous.
04 / Users and workflows

One workspace, different depths of investigation

Portfolio managers primarily need rapid construction, understandable trade-offs, and stable comparisons. Analysts and researchers need precise assumptions, look-through detail, multi-factor inspection, and inspectable methodology. Proposed They use the same interface; the distinction is how far they drill, rather than a separate role-specific product. Permissions may control access, but this document does not invent a permissions system.

PM · Build a new allocation

Story: As a portfolio manager, I want to start blank, from a template, or from an existing portfolio/import so that construction starts from the most useful context.

Flow: choose an origin → name the draft → add sleeves and investments with universal search → edit weights → inspect live analytics → add references or alternatives.

Acceptance: all three origins are equally discoverable; the draft opens in the same workspace; incomplete weights remain editable; the user sees the total and current data coverage without a blocking “complete setup” step.

PM · Modify an existing allocation

Story: As a portfolio manager, I want an editable working draft with immediate analytical feedback so that I can understand the effect of a weight change.

Flow: select the active draft → expand its sleeve → edit a numeric weight → observe affected cells briefly highlight → inspect the selected row chart.

Acceptance: the edit changes the active draft directly; autosave status is visible; the UI does not create an unsolicited scenario copy; a valid result is associated with the latest committed edit.

PM / analyst · Compare alternatives

Story: As a user, I want four to six allocations aligned in one table so that I can compare drivers and benchmark differences without mentally joining separate screens.

Flow: add live allocations or fixed snapshots → select metric and time basis → inspect contributions → switch to differences → choose shared or individual references.

Acceptance: switching to differences preserves row context; every reference is named; unavailable values are not displayed as zero; factor selection is shared across all allocations.

Researcher · Test assumptions

Story: As a researcher, I want to change returns, variances, betas, and correlations at several levels so that I can inspect sensitivity to alternative assumptions.

Flow: open scenario drawer → select portfolio scope → find and edit matrix cells → inspect base/scenario results side by side → inspect projection or scenario charts.

Acceptance: overridden values are identifiable; base values remain inspectable; out-of-scope allocations remain unchanged; invalid input is flagged precisely; scenario work autosaves to this workspace.

Analyst · Explain a driver

Story: As an analyst, I want to expand a sleeve and inspect its children so that I can explain a contribution without losing the parent context.

Flow: select a high-level row → expand into sub-portfolios or holdings → choose return, risk, weight, or factors → inspect the chart below → adjust legend emphasis.

Acceptance: the table retains a coherent hierarchy; parent totals are not double counted as additional children; the chart title names the selected row, metric, unit, and relevant period.

Analyst · Understand a data limitation

Story: As an analyst, I want to see whether a value uses actual history, a backtest, a proxy, or incomplete coverage so that I can judge its suitability.

Flow: inspect a basis or coverage label → open context → identify affected holdings and periods → continue analysis using the available values.

Acceptance: limitations appear close to affected results; missingness and true zero are distinct; mixed bases are visible across comparison columns rather than hidden in a global footnote.

05 / Construction contract

Fast numeric editing with explicit portfolio context

The construction surface is an expandable sleeve tree, not a visual allocation wheel or a slider-led editor. Rows identify their object type—sleeve, existing sub-portfolio, fund, index, security, or other supported investment—and expose precise numeric weights. Universal search spans supported investment types with clear labels so that similarly named objects are distinguishable.

Inline editing and residuals

Trigger: user edits a weight. State: an input may temporarily contain an unfinished number; a committed numeric value updates the draft. Output: total allocation and unallocated/residual amount update, followed by eligible analytics.

Edge condition: totals need not equal 100%. Do not automatically normalize other rows or block exploration. A residual must not silently be labelled or priced as cash unless that treatment is explicitly selected.

Editing 40% to 35% leaves the five-percentage-point residual visible and does not rescale unrelated holdings.

Short and leveraged allocations

Trigger: enable support for the active portfolio. Output: negative and leveraged weights become available, with gross exposure, net exposure, and borrowing shown as informational metrics.

Edge condition: do not impose risk limits, breach banners, recommended limits, or approval gates for leverage. Type, numeric-range, and model-input validation are distinct from risk controls and remain necessary where the analytical input cannot be interpreted.

Once enabled, a valid short weight or net allocation above 100% is not rejected because of a risk policy. Information labels explain their basis without making a recommendation.

Nested weight semantics

Proposed Show both parent-relative and total-portfolio weight on demand. Editing a parent sleeve preserves proportions within it unless a different redistribution rule is explicitly chosen.

Open decision: which weight is directly editable by default; how residuals are represented inside each sleeve; whether a reused sub-portfolio is a linked object or a local copy.

A child’s displayed unit includes its basis. Editing a reusable portfolio cannot silently mutate unrelated workspaces.

Search and import

Search: result rows show the investment name, type, and enough distinguishing metadata to choose the correct object. Adding an item should preserve the active sleeve and current analysis view.

Proposed Imports provide a compact preview for identifier matches, weight basis, units, dates, and unmatched rows. Retain incomplete entries for correction; do not silently drop them or assume they have zero weight.

The user sees which rows were added, require mapping, or lack analytical coverage. The interface can explain why a new holding is not yet reflected in a result.

Try the builder and comparison example. The placeholder calculation exists to demonstrate feedback and state transitions; production residual, financing, and derivatives semantics remain domain inputs to be finalized.

06 / Drivers & Differences

One table with stable rows and explicit analytical lenses

Keep the table structure stable while the user changes the question. Historical versus forward-looking determines the analytical time basis. Contributions versus differences determines whether values are absolute or relative. The metric selector chooses weight, return contribution, risk contribution, or factor exposure. Row expansion follows sleeves and holdings; factors also have their own analytical dimension and must not be presented as if they were children of every sleeve.

Metric contracts

  • Allocation weight: display a clearly stated portfolio or parent basis. Differences are percentage-point differences, not relative percentage changes.
  • Return contribution: display contributions for the selected period or forecast horizon. Label annualized versus cumulative conventions.
  • Risk contribution: switch between share of total risk and contribution to portfolio volatility. Do not treat variance contribution and volatility contribution as interchangeable.
  • Factor exposure: use a shared factor picker. Each selected factor appears within every allocation group with its own declared unit.

A change of metric changes headings, formatting, tooltips, and chart units together; stale labels do not persist.

Reference modes

Shared reference: one benchmark or chosen portfolio supplies the reference for all comparison columns. Individual references: each allocation is evaluated against its own assigned benchmark.

State: the reference control and each relevant column header identify the effective reference. Switching modes preserves contributions and only changes the relative view.

Edge condition: if an allocation lacks an individual benchmark, show “No benchmark assigned” and unavailable differences—not zero or a silent fallback.

Changing the reference changes the difference calculation and its label together. A portfolio compared with itself yields zero where both values are available.

Hierarchy and different structures

Expansion reveals child sleeves, sub-portfolios, and holdings. Parent and child presentation must explain aggregation, residuals, and any non-additive measure.

Proposed A common asset-class grouping can align portfolios with different native structures. Native hierarchy remains available. A holding absent from a portfolio is zero only when absence is known; unavailable data is marked separately.

Expanding a row does not shift columns out of alignment or duplicate a parent subtotal in a grand total.

Factors and width management

The user chooses the same set of factors across all allocations. Factors appear together rather than one at a time. Sticky row labels and horizontally scrollable grouped headers preserve orientation as factor count increases.

Edge condition: factor units can differ. Do not imply that an equity beta and a rate sensitivity can be compared numerically without their definitions. Missing model exposure is “Unavailable,” not a neutral zero.

Adding a factor adds it to every allocation group. Removing it removes all corresponding columns while preserving selection of the other factors.

Side-by-side scenarios: when a scenario is active, pair Base and Scenario values within each relevant allocation and metric context. This introduces density deliberately; allow horizontal scrolling and compact labels. The Contributions/Differences switch still refers to the selected benchmark or portfolio reference, while a scenario comparison explicitly identifies its Base versus Scenario relationship.

07 / Historical analysis and cross-asset coherence

Comparable views without disguising different data bases

Historical growth of investment leads the historical chart experience. The default date range is the common usable history across compared allocations. Use actual portfolio history when complete position/weight history is available; otherwise backtest the current weights, with quarterly rebalancing as the default rule. Every comparison must show which basis it uses.

Actual history versus current-weight backtest

Trigger: load or modify an allocation. Output: a basis label identifies Actual history or Current-weight backtest. A methodology detail states the period and rebalance convention.

Edge condition: “complete history” requires a defined completeness test. Partial positions, missing valuation periods, and incomplete transactions must not be silently presented as actual performance.

Open decision: when a user changes today’s weights in an allocation with actual history, decide whether the historical view preserves actual history, switches to a backtest for the edited draft, or exposes both. A badge must make the chosen behavior explicit.

A comparison containing actual and backtested columns visibly identifies both. A backtest does not appear as a realized track record.

Common inception and date controls

Default: start at the latest eligible inception among the compared allocations, using one common evaluation window.

Proposed Expose date presets and custom dates. Explain a shortened range caused by a newly added allocation. Preserve the user’s selected range where possible, but report missing coverage rather than extrapolating.

Adding a short-history portfolio updates the displayed date range and explains the change. Removing it does not silently alter an explicitly chosen range.

Unified multi-asset presentation

Use consistent labels and controls for weights, contribution, risk, factors, and references across major asset classes. Underlying differences in valuation frequency, proxy usage, currency treatment, model coverage, and lag remain visible through data context.

The interface must not imply daily observed accuracy for monthly or quarterly-valued investments. The common analytical framework does not authorize inventing correlations, values, or exposures for unsupported assets.

Illiquid and frequently valued assets can coexist in the view; their basis and freshness remain inspectable at row or portfolio level.

Coverage, units, and availability

Proposed Show coverage summaries and on-demand source, as-of date, and proxy information. Keep true zero, no exposure, stale data, partial coverage, and unavailable calculation as distinct states.

Open decisions: reporting currency and hedging convention; return net/gross-of-fee convention; observation frequency; historical factor coverage; annualization; handling of asynchronous valuations; portfolio-level benchmark inheritance into sleeves.

A numeric value is accompanied by enough context to understand its basis. A disabled metric explains why it is unavailable and preserves the rest of the workspace.

08 / Assumption editing

A matrix for researchers, immediate results for everyone

The platform model provides the base assumptions. Users may temporarily override expected returns, variances or volatilities, betas, correlations, and other supported model inputs at asset-class, factor, and holding levels. The interface exposes these as scenario assumptions; it does not edit the underlying platform model. Scenarios remain local to the current workspace and autosave there.

Matrix editing

Trigger: open the drawer and select a level and assumption type. State: editable matrix cells show current scenario values, units, and overridden status. Output: a committed valid edit recalculates eligible scenario outputs for the chosen scope.

Proposed Support search, filtering to overridden cells, copy/paste, per-cell reset, grouped reset, and a complete scenario reset.

Editing a percentage in a cell cannot be mistaken for entering its decimal representation. Variance and volatility are clearly differentiated and cannot share an ambiguous “Risk” heading.

Correlation matrix plus pair editor

Use a full matrix to scan relationships and a focused pair editor to inspect or alter one relationship precisely. Selection is synchronized: selecting a matrix cell identifies the same pair in the editor.

Proposed Mirror changes across symmetric cells; make the diagonal non-editable where the model requires it. Show values and labels in addition to colour intensity.

Editing A–B updates B–A consistently. Out-of-range or nonnumeric values remain identifiable and do not produce seemingly valid refreshed analytics.

Scope and base comparison

Default application scope is all compared allocations; users can select a subset. Results show Base and Scenario side by side. Portfolio and scenario selection are separate state dimensions.

Edge condition: an asset-level override may affect multiple holdings or portfolios through shared exposures. The effective scope must remain explicit. An allocation outside scope stays on its base or previously selected assumption set.

Changing scope never changes construction weights. Out-of-scope outputs do not animate as though they were recalculated under the new scenario.

Precedence and mathematical validity

Proposed Show effective value, inherited value, source level, and active override where inputs overlap. Retain last valid results while invalid inputs are being corrected, with an unmistakable stale/invalid status.

Open decisions: precedence between asset, factor, and holding overrides; dependencies among beta, variance, and correlation edits; and how a non-positive-semidefinite correlation matrix is handled. The UI must not silently repair a model input or substitute values.

Users can identify which value the scenario uses. A model validation failure identifies affected assumptions and does not overwrite the last valid base case.

The scenario example demonstrates the interaction with placeholder data. Multiple named scenarios, duplication, and version recovery are proposed extensions of the confirmed workspace-only storage choice—not previously confirmed preferences.

09 / Charts linked to the table

Use the chart to explain the selected row.

The chart is below the table and follows the selected row. It is not an unrelated dashboard widget. Its title must identify the sleeve or holding, current metric, unit, and date or forecast horizon. Every compared allocation remains represented, with emphasis controlled through the chart legend. Legend selection supports a set of allocations, not only one focused series.

Historical chart

Growth of investment is the initial historical visualization. When the table selects a different supported metric, show its appropriate historical series with the same allocation colours and chart emphasis.

Proposed A shared crosshair reports aligned values at one date. Missing observations create disclosed gaps rather than invented interpolation. Preserve emphasis when the user changes the selected row or metric.

Selecting a row changes the chart’s label and data together; the chart never presents the previous row’s series under the new title.

Forward-looking projection

A Projection/Scenarios switch selects the chart’s purpose. Projection offers Standalone growth and Portfolio contribution. Standalone illustrates a common starting investment in a sleeve; contribution reflects the sleeve’s share of the starting portfolio.

Example: a 20% sleeve with 30% cumulative growth is 100 → 130 standalone, or 20 → 26 within a portfolio initially worth 100. Labels must identify the starting basis and currency.

Changing the projection basis updates the scale and labels while leaving the underlying allocation and assumptions unchanged.

Uncertainty and small charts

Show a central path and uncertainty bands. Each emphasized allocation receives a separate small chart with matching axes so bands remain distinguishable. Other allocations stay available through the legend/context rather than becoming unrelated or unidentifiable.

Proposed Use one horizon and one confidence setting across the charts. The precise default confidence level, central statistic, and forecast interpretation must be agreed before production.

All small charts use comparable units and scales. Band labels identify probability coverage and horizon; a band is not presented as a guaranteed range.

Scenario chart

Show the selected row’s result under the base case and each included workspace scenario, with allocation and scenario identities distinct. The chart reflects scope: not every scenario necessarily applies to every allocation.

Edge condition: too many scenario/allocation combinations require explicit selection or scrolling, not colour reuse that hides identity. Unavailable combinations remain labelled.

The user can map a plotted scenario value to the matching table result and assumption set. Switching chart mode does not change the working scenario.

Try the chart example to see selection, legend emphasis, projection basis, and the use of separate panels.

10 / Live behavior, persistence, and versions

Fast feedback requires trustworthy state.

Autosave and live updates remove explicit Save and Run steps from routine work. They also create obligations: users must know what is saved, which inputs produced a result, and whether the displayed output is current. A brief changed-cell highlight provides orientation after recalculation; it should not become a permanent before/after comparison mode.

Input and calculation lifecycle

Proposed Distinguish Editing → Saving/Updating → Current, with separate Error states. Commit parseable values at a stable edit boundary; avoid a disruptive recalculation for an incomplete keystroke such as a lone minus sign.

Keep the previous successful output visible while updating and label it accordingly. Results must be tied to the input version that produced them; a slower older response must not replace a newer one.

Rapidly making three edits cannot leave the screen displaying the first edit’s result as current. The active input remains focused when values refresh.

Continuous save

Confirmed: drafts and workspace scenarios autosave continuously. Proposed: visible states include Saving, Saved, and Not saved—retry available. Save failures are not disguised as successful persistence.

Open decision: persistence boundaries and multi-user conflict handling. The UI must not claim that a change is shared or remotely stored solely because local state has updated.

Leaving and reopening a successfully saved workspace restores its saved data. A failed save retains the working input and communicates the failure.

Undo and recovery

Proposed Provide undo/redo for construction and assumption edits, with appropriate grouping for a pasted block or moved holding. Version history enables restoration beyond a single session.

Edge condition: restoring a draft state should create a traceable new current state rather than erasing fixed snapshots. Clarify whether layout changes belong in the same undo history as investment inputs.

Undo returns both the edited input and its analytical context to the intended prior state, then recalculates and autosaves.

Live allocations and snapshots

Comparisons support both. Live allocations reflect their current saved state; a snapshot retains the captured allocation/version. Their labels must remain distinguishable.

Proposed A snapshot is read-only and can be used to create a draft. Open decision: whether snapshot reproducibility also freezes model version, market data, assumption set, benchmark, and dates. Do not promise reproducible analytics from frozen weights alone.

Editing a live allocation does not change the stored weights of a snapshot. Any refreshed analytical basis on a snapshot is explicitly identified.

See the live-state example. Placeholder browser storage demonstrates the pattern; it is not a production persistence architecture.

11 / Interaction quality

A dense desktop interface must remain operable and readable.

Keyboard and focus

Proposed All controls, row expansion, numeric editing, factor selection, references, chart legend items, and drawer controls are keyboard-operable. Use predictable focus order and visible focus indicators. Opening a drawer moves focus intentionally; closing it returns focus to the trigger.

A user can edit a weight, expand a sleeve, change metric, open a scenario, and close it using only the keyboard. Resizing has a keyboard-accessible alternative.

Tables and charts

Expose row and column headers, grouped allocation/factor headers, expanded state, and selected state semantically. Numeric alignment, consistent precision, and unit labels make comparisons readable. Colour supplements labels and icons rather than conveying the only distinction.

An assistive technology user can identify the allocation, scenario, factor, unit, and row for a value. Chart data remains available in an accessible table or equivalent summary.

Motion and change

Changed-cell highlighting is brief, subtle, and non-directional: a change is not automatically good or bad. Honour reduced-motion preferences. Announce completed updates without reading every changed numeric cell aloud.

Updates do not steal focus, scroll the table unexpectedly, or animate continuously. Missing-data and input-error signals are distinguishable from changed-value highlights.

Responsive behavior and empty states

The primary target is a very large desktop monitor. On narrower screens, preserve analytical meaning through collapsible panels and local scrolling instead of compressing all columns. Empty, loading, unavailable, invalid, and failed states receive specific text and a relevant recovery action.

At a narrower width, core actions remain reachable and headings are not cropped into ambiguous fragments. “No results” is not used to represent every failure state.

Quality review: verify a four-to-six-allocation comparison, a deep sleeve hierarchy, a wide factor set, simultaneous scenario editing, interrupted saving, missing historical coverage, a short-enabled portfolio, and keyboard-only operation. These are product behavior checks, not a prescribed technology stack.
12 / Proposed delivery sequence

Protect the complete analytical loop first.

Proposed—not confirmed The following ordering is an implementation recommendation for review, not an agreed schedule. It preserves equal importance for creating, modifying, and comparing allocations. A feature’s priority reflects whether it is required for that loop to be trustworthy and usable, not whether its analytics are intellectually interesting.

P0 · Essential coherent workspace

  • Adjustable builder and combined Drivers & Differences table.
  • Expandable sleeves, inline weights, universal search, and blank/template/clone-import entry paths.
  • Live updates, continuous saving with honest status, incomplete allocations, and explicit short/leverage enablement.
  • Four-to-six comparisons; contribution/difference and historical/forward-looking switches; four selected metrics; shared factor selection; reference modes.
  • Historical basis labels, common inception, quarterly current-weight backtest convention, data/coverage states.
  • Scenario drawer with core assumption edits, selectable scope, hybrid correlation editing, and Base/Scenario results.
  • Selected-row chart, legend emphasis, accessible controls, and essential error recovery.

Why first: together these form a complete build → understand → compare → test loop. Removing a whole workflow would contradict the equal-priority brief.

P1 · Deeper investigation and reuse

  • Projection and scenario chart modes, both projection bases, central paths and small-chart bands.
  • Richer matrix productivity: bulk paste, override filters, inherited-value detail, and grouped resets.
  • Multiple named workspace scenarios and duplication.
  • Snapshot creation and restoration workflows with explicit reproducibility scope; deeper edit history.
  • Common grouping for structurally different portfolios, advanced search/sorting, and comprehensive data provenance detail.

Why next: these improve depth and research speed after the core semantics are reliable. Projection remains part of the target experience; deferral is a delivery proposal, not removal.

P2 · Polish and distribution

  • Exports of the visible analytical state with complete labels and context.
  • Extended layout preference restoration and advanced table customization.
  • Additional shortcuts and sophisticated multi-panel layout behavior.
  • Expanded edge-case import assistance and optional presentation refinements.

Why later: these should not delay a usable analytical loop. If external communication or import volume is a core release requirement, move the relevant item earlier.

Explicitly outside this front-end brief

  • Investment recommendations, trade proposals, or optimizer-driven advice.
  • Trading, order execution, approval workflows, or leverage risk limits.
  • A cross-workspace scenario library.
  • Designing the platform’s capital-market model, data ingestion services, or forecasting algorithm.
  • Unrequested collaboration and entitlement architecture.

Front-end representation of calculation status, validation, units, provenance, and missing data remains in scope even where the underlying service is not.

Decisions to finalize before engineering treats semantics as fixed
  1. Weight basis and residuals: parent versus total weights, parent-edit redistribution, cash versus unallocated treatment, financing, and nested residuals.
  2. Editable current weights versus actual history: what the historical view shows after an allocation with a real track record is edited.
  3. Model metrics: exact return and risk contribution conventions, annualization, factor units, residual attribution, and historical availability.
  4. Override semantics: hierarchy precedence, interdependent beta/variance/correlation inputs, and invalid covariance handling without silent adjustment.
  5. Benchmark inheritance: which benchmark applies to a sleeve or holding and how references with different holdings are aligned.
  6. Snapshot and baseline scope: whether inputs, assumption versions, model versions, market data, benchmarks, and periods are frozen or refreshed.
  7. Multi-asset comparability: currency and hedging treatment, fees, valuation frequency, illiquid assets, proxies, and incomplete imports.
  8. Projection definition: horizon defaults, central statistic, confidence level, and weighted-growth treatment under rebalancing.
  9. Workspace persistence: private versus shared state, save conflicts, refresh behavior, and which preferences are local to a user.
  10. Release scope: confirm or revise P0/P1/P2 after feasibility and user review. No schedule or effort estimate is implied.
13 / Full assumption traceability

The 50 predicted answers remain visible and reviewable.

These are the additional questions answered by prediction during the interview. Each remains a proposed assumption, even when used to make an interactive example concrete. Items marked “lower confidence” were explicitly identified as such in the prediction. Requirements above distinguish these from direct user selections.

01–10 · Scenario editing and management
  1. Multiple named scenarios? Yes, retain alternative assumptions within the workspace alongside the base case. Multiple scenarios were not yet answered directly.
  2. Duplicate a scenario? Yes, copy an existing scenario and change a small number of inputs.
  3. Keep scenario context visible when the drawer closes? Yes, use a compact scenario name and override count.
  4. Show only overridden assumptions? Yes, provide an “Overrides only” matrix filter.
  5. Reset assumptions at different scopes? Yes, per cell, selected group, or complete scenario.
  6. Show each original platform value? Yes, alongside the override or on selection.
  7. Handle invalid assumptions? Flag affected cells and retain last valid results while the input is corrected.
  8. Explain conflicting overrides? Show effective value and precedence; the precedence rule itself remains unresolved. Lower confidence.
  9. Paste blocks of assumptions? Yes, with individual identification of invalid entries.
  10. Drawer overlay or resize? Prefer an adjustable drawer that resizes the analytical canvas. Lower confidence.
11–20 · Scope, state, and comparison membership
  1. Independent scenario and portfolio selection? Yes; comparison membership and scenario application are separate controls.
  2. Change allocations outside scenario scope? No; retain their existing assumption context and label it.
